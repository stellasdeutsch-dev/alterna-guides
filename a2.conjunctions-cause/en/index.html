02 Guess first

Guess before the rule

“I was late because the bus didn't come.” Which zipper do you need here? Take a guess. Mistakes are free. Hint: look at what comes first — the fact or the cause.

03 The çünkü model
#1
Fact first, then “because”
Geç kaldımçünküotobüs gelmedi

çünkü — “because.” Put it between the result and the cause. First you say what happened. Then — why.

Same order as English “because,” and no comma before it either. That's why people pick up çünkü in an evening.

Say it right: ç is “ch” in “church,” ü is “ee” said with rounded lips. çünkü — “chün-kü,” never “kun-ku.”

One catch: English can start with “Because…”. çünkü can't open a sentence before the result. More on that below.

04 The bu yüzden model
#2
Cause first, then “so”
Otobüs gelmedibu yüzdengeç kaldım

bu yüzden — “so” or “that's why.” Literally “because of this”: bu = this, yüz = face, -den = from. Like English “so,” it takes a comma before it. The zipper runs the other way: cause first, then result.

In speech, bu yüzden often shows up when the result is unpleasant: late, soaked, didn't buy it. But that's a habit, not a law.

If you hear “so” in your head, go with bu yüzden.

06 Question
#3
Neden? — Çünkü…
The question is asked with neden. The answer starts with çünkü.

Asking “why?” — you say neden (“neh-den”). In conversation there's also niye (“nee-yeh”). No flipping words like in “why are you…” — just put neden into a normal sentence.

You can start your answer right with çünkü. Not in writing, but in speech — all the time.

Turks also like answering with one word and a pause. Don't do that. Remember how it went with the jacket.

07 Collection

Three models for the news

bu yüzden has siblings: bu nedenle, bu sebeple, bunun için. They all share one order: cause, connector, result.

English has the same ladder: so — that's why — therefore — for this reason. bunun için ≈ “that's why,” bu nedenle ≈ “therefore,” bu sebeple ≈ “for this reason.” Hoodie versus blazer: same meaning, different occasion. Tap a model to hear an example.

Chatting with a friend, one bu yüzden is plenty. The rest come in handy for the news, emails and exams.

09 Seams

Where to put the comma

Before çünkü there's usually no comma. Before bu yüzden — a comma or a period. Same habit as English “because” and “so.” Tap a card — the seam gets stitched.

The comma is the seam between the halves. One zipper hides it, the other shows it. Mix them up and the meaning survives, but the eye snags.
