Conditions in every tense

What if…one letter ruins your evening?

Turkish can say “if” in any tense: almışsa, geliyorsa, gelecekse. And, separately, “if only”: bilseydim. Mix them up — and your friend takes someone else to the concert.

Module 01

One “if only” — and the evening's gone

Samsun, Friday evening. There's a concert on the waterfront, and Liza is home with tea and Turkish.

Her friend Eda texts: Bu akşam sahilde konser var. Geliyor musun? Concert on the waterfront tonight. You coming?

Liza wants to reply: “If you've already bought tickets — I'll come.” She writes: Bilet alsaydın gelirdim.

Eda reads: “If you had bought them, I would have come.” So you didn't — I'm not coming. The reply: Ama aldım zaten! Neyse, Emre'yi çağırırım. But I did buy them! Fine, I'll ask Emre.

Liza watches the fireworks over the sea from her window. One “if only” — and the evening went to Emre.

Module 02

A tag that sticks to everything

You already know plain -sA. Gelirsen sevinirim. If you come, I'll be glad. Keşke gelseydin. I wish you'd come.

Now a C1-level trick. There's a word ise — “if that's so.” It shrinks into the tag -(y)sA.

The tag sticks to any tense. Almış + ise = almışsa. Geliyor + ise = geliyorsa. The tense stays, “if” gets added. English does the same — but its “if” stands in front, and Turkish glues it to the end.

Harmony looks at the last vowel of the form: -sa or -se. If the form ends in a vowel, add y: gelmeliyse.

Module 03

If already and if right now

Guess first. Ev sahibi mektubu almışsa cevap verir. Does Liza know if the letter arrived?

She doesn't. -mışsa means “if he's already gotten it, and I didn't see it.” A bet on the past.

Tramvay geliyorsa koşalım! If the tram's already coming — let's run. -yorsa holds the present.

The rule: tense + -(y)sA = “if that's true.” The door is open, anything can still happen.

What does Liza know about the letter?

Mektubu almışsa cevap verir. If he got it, he'll reply.

Module 04

If you're going to and if you have to

Saturday. Eda might drop by to make up. Eda gelecekse pasta alalım. If Eda's going to come, let's buy a cake.

-ecekse looks ahead: the plan isn't real yet, but we're getting ready for it. The cake's already in the oven.

Eda came, but she's in a hurry. Liza: Gitmeliysen git, tutmuyorum. If you have to go — go, I'm not keeping you.

-meliyse — “if you have to.” The person goes at the very end: gelmeliysem, gelmeliysen.

Module 05

If only I'd known in advance

Friday again. The fireworks are over. Liza whispers: Önceden bilseydim gelirdim. If I'd known in advance, I would have come.

-saydı is “if only.” The door is already shut: didn't know, didn't come.

The second half is in the past too: -(ı)rdı. Erken çıksaydım tramvayı kaçırmazdım. If I'd left earlier, I wouldn't have missed the tram.

The rule: verb + -sAydI … -(I)rdI = “if I had… I would have.” Regret in two words. -(I)rdI is your “would”: gelirdim — I would have come.

Can this still happen?

Bileti almışsan gelirim. If you've already bought the ticket — I'll come.

Common mistakes

English speakers trip over these. Four cards: the mistake on the front, the fix on the back. Tap “Fix.”

Meant “if already”

Meant: “If you've already bought the tickets, I'll come.”

Bilet alsaydın gelirdim.

Correct

Bileti almışsan gelirim.

-saydı is “if you had…” — you didn't, so I'm not coming. “If you've already bought” is a live “if”: put the tag on -mış.

Harmony by root

“If you're coming now.”

Geliyorsen

Correct

Geliyorsan

English has no vowel harmony, so it's easy to forget. The tag looks at the last vowel of the whole form, not the root. In geliyor that's o — so -sa.

“Eğer” instead of the tag

“If the tram's coming, let's run!”

Eğer tramvay geliyor, koşalım!

Correct

Eğer tramvay geliyorsa koşalım!

Eğer looks like English “if,” but it's optional. The real “if” is the tag: geliyor-sa. Without it, the sentence says “the tram is coming.”

“Had” turned into -dı

“If I had known, I would have come.”

Önceden bildiysem gelirdim.

Correct

Önceden bilseydim gelirdim.

Bildiysem is a real word, but it means “if I did know” — maybe I did. For something that didn't happen: -se first, then -ydi: bil-se-ydi-m.
