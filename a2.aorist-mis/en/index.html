03

remember

Half of it is already done

The first ending is the aorist. You know it. Quick reminder.

✓Ends in a vowel — just -r: oku → okur.

✓One syllable — -ar/-er: yap → yapar. Except the thirteen: gel → gelir.

✓Two syllables or more — -ır/-ir/-ur/-ür: çalış → çalışır.

04

rule

Habit + hearsay = legend

-Ar — The Habit. Does the same thing every day.

-mIş — The Gossip. Didn’t see it, but will tell you all about it.

The habit sits closer to the verb. The gossip comes after it.

Together you get a legend: someone always did this, and you only know it secondhand.

  1. The verb yap — “to do.”
  2. Habit: yap-ar. “Usually does.”
  3. Gossip: yap-ar-mış. “They say he used to do.”
  4. Done. One word — a whole family story.

05

harmony

The gossip copies the habit

-mIş doesn’t pick its own vowel. It borrows it from the neighbor on the left.

Whatever vowel is in -Ar, the same one goes into -mIş. Four cells — four options.

The gossip is a simple creature. It repeats what it heard.

Tap a cell. The vowel will jump over on its own.

06

table

Six persons, one pattern

The person ending goes at the very end, after the gossip. The endings are the same as with -mIş.

Nothing new to learn.

One detail. With “they,” the plural comes earlier: yapar-lar-mış.

“They say we used to go for a walk every day” — yürü…

07

negative and question

They say he never…

The habit has its own negative: -mAz. The gossip sticks to it.

Dedem hiç sigara içmezmiş. They say grandpa never smoked.

Questions are rare. We write mI separately, and the gossip moves over to it: içer miymiş?

People usually ask this about other people’s grandpas. About your own, they’ve already told you everything.

“They say grandpa was never late” — geç kal…

09

four uses

Where this form lives

Four places. Same thing everywhere: a habit you know from what people say.

The fourth use is sneaky. A rumor can be about today, too. The bakery is open, the boyoz is hot, and you still haven’t tried it.

10

English ↔ Turkish

Three words in one ending

In English, a legend is built from pieces: they say, usually, got up.

Turkish hides it all in the ending of one verb. “Usually” is -ar. “They say” is -mış.

In Turkish you don’t even have to say “they say.” The ending already said it.

Dedem kalkarmış
