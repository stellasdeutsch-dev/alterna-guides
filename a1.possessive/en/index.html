Notice? Everything “mine” ends in m. That’s the owner’s signature. Linguists call it a possessive suffix.

Like a stamp on a document: without it, the thing belongs to nobody.

rule

Three signatures

The owner is me, you or him. Each has its own signature. It sticks to the end of the word. We’ll get to the linking vowel and the buffer below — for now, watch the signature.

evim
çantam

Take a thing.

  • m — mine. The most common signature in life.
  • n — yours. You say it to a friend, a waiter, a cat.
  • ı — his or hers. Turkish has no gender, one signature for everyone.

infographic

Four linking vowels

The linking vowel adapts to the word’s last vowel. There are four options. Tap a sector. Tap the cells too — every form plays.

Word ends in a vowel? No linking vowel: çantam, kedin. The high vowel only flies in after a consonant.

You already know the linking vowel from harmony. A back word gets a back linking vowel, a front word gets a front one. Rounded lips — the linking vowel is rounded too.

compare

Where “my” lives

In English, “my” stands in front as a separate word. In Turkish it moves to the tail and sticks. You say “my” a hundred times a day. Now you’ll say it with a tail.

myhouse

English: the owner is a separate word in front.

Lots of languages keep the owner up front, English included: my house. Turkish is closer to Kazakh here. Know Kazakh? Then this is familiar: “my house” is üiım. Same tail, same logic.

special case

The buffer letter

Kedi plus ı. Two vowels bump heads. Turkish doesn’t like that. Between them stands s, like a polite doorman. Without it you’d get kedii — a sound like someone hiccuping into the phone.

kedi → his cat

The doorman works only one door. The buffer is only for “his” and “her.” My and your get by with one consonant: kedim, kedin.

algorithm

Three questions

Ask the questions in order. The dot runs along the chart and builds the form.

  1. Who’s the owner? Me — m, you — n, him or her — a vowel.
  2. How does the word end? Vowel or consonant.
  3. If a consonant — what’s the last vowel? It picks the linking vowel.
mnemonic

My new suitcase

Three words — three signatures. The first letters are the endings.

-m-n-sı

My — that’s you: “mine.” New — for you: “yours.” Suitcase — already someone else’s: “his.”

The word is the thing. The ending is its passport.
