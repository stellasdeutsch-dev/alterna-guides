“As if” in Turkish

“How much truth there is depends on how honestly you say ‘as if.’” Turkish says it three ways.

Render, photo, and forecast. Today you’ll learn to tell them apart by ear.

Story

An apartment just like the photos

Samsun. Atakum. You’re looking for a place for a month. You find a listing. The photos are a magazine spread. White sofa, globe lamps, the sea in the window.

You message the owner. You say, is it really like that? He says, just like the photos.

You show up. The door opens with a sound decent homes don’t make. You’re standing on the set of a movie about the nineties.

You say, where’s the sea? He says, right there. Points at a painting. The sea in the painting is great, by the way.

The owner didn’t lie. He said “as if.” You just didn’t catch the ending.

What does “yeni yapılmış gibi” mean?

Guess first

As he said, or as if he said?

Two sentences. One difference in the middle. Where did the action really happen?

Hint: you already know -DIğI. The newcomer here is -mış. Getting it wrong is fine — it’s a warm-up.

Rule

-mış gibi is a render

A render looks like a real apartment. But it’s a picture. Same with -mış gibi: it looks like the truth, but it isn’t.

Beni gördü

He saw me. Fact.

  1. Fact: beni gördü — he saw me.
  2. Swap the tense for -mış: görmüş.
  3. Add gibi — and you get “as if.”
  4. Negative: görmemiş gibi — as if he hadn’t seen.
verb+-mIş+gibi=“as if he did”

how true it is

“As if” almost always means: that’s not how it really is. The speaker knows it. Now so do you.

Next usually comes a frame verb: davranmak, yapmak, görünmek, hissetmek. More on that below.

Beni görmemiş gibi geçti. Did he see me?

Persons and nouns

Who was pretending

The person ending goes after -mış, before gibi. You can drop it if the sentence makes it clear.

Third person has no ending: just anlamış gibi. The most common form in speech.

The ending helps when there are lots of characters. “Anlamışım gibi” — I’m the one who nodded. Not the neighbor, not the cat.

Nouns and adjectives work the same way, with -(y)mış. After a vowel you need y.

Tense

As if it was, is, or will be

You can put a tense before -mış. That gives you three floors of the same render.

-mış itself always comes last before gibi. Tense and negation come earlier.

But -acak gibi without -mış is no longer a render. It’s a forecast: the signs are real.

Yağmur yağacak gibi.Looks like it’s about to rain.

Ağlayacak gibi oldu.He almost cried.

The sky is black, thunder rumbles. How do you say “looks like rain”?

Photo vs render

You know -DIğI gibi — “as.” That’s a photo: it all really happened. -mış gibi is a render. One letter, and the truth turns into a show.

photo · as is
render · as if

Remember the pair. “Dediğin gibi” — you said it. “Demişsin gibi” — as if you said it. One syllable apart, and a whole reputation.

A photo doesn’t argue. A render always looks good.

Stucco

-cAsInA — the literary “as if”

With adjectives it’s even simpler: çılgın — crazy, çılgıncasına — like crazy. No -mış needed.

Same meaning, just with stucco on top. Instead of gibi — the ending -casına / -cesine. This is how novels and essays write it.

Hiçbir şey olmamış gibi gülümsedi.

He smiled as if nothing had happened.

In a chat with a friend, -casına looks like a tailcoat at the beach. Save it for books.

After ş and other voiceless consonants — ç. The vowel follows the word’s last vowel.

Frames

Five frames for a render

Each frame tells you where the “as if” comes from: behavior, looks, or feelings.

After gibi there’s almost always a frame verb. Pick it by meaning, like finishes by budget.

gibi yapmakto pretend
gibi davranmakto act as if
gibi görünmekto look as if
gibi hissetmekto feel as if
gibi gelmekto seem

The word sanki adds weight: “as though.” Put it at the start. Without a frame, “as if” just hangs in the air, like a chandelier with no ceiling.

Memory trick

Mouse gibi

Hear mış gibi — picture a mouse. It sits very still and pretends it isn’t there: yokmuş gibi.

Mış and “mouse” — close enough if you squint. Coincidence? Not for your memory.

The mouse is always there. It’s just pretending.

A month later you move out. The apartment shines. You scrubbed it so hard it started looking like a render.

The landlord takes photos for a new listing. He says, like a magazine. You say, uh-huh.

Ev hiç yaşanmamış gibi görünüyor.

The apartment looks as if no one ever lived there.

That last sentence is a render too. The apartment looks unlived-in. Even though you lived there a month and know every creak.

Now you can hear the difference between a photo, a render, and a forecast. In Turkish, on the first try.
