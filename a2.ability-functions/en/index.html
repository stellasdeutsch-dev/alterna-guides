Çeşme. Heat. Lifeguard.

You’re on the beach in Çeşme. The water looks like an ad. There’s a flag on the tower. You want to swim. You walk up to the lifeguard. You speak Turkish. You surprise yourself.

The lifeguard answers. Three phrases. You nod at each one. You got one thing: every one has -ebil.

What did the lifeguard do in the second phrase?

He gave permission. Warned. Gave advice. With the same tail. By the end of the lesson you’ll see which flag is up.

form

Four blocks

Same form as last lesson. Verb. Tail -abil or -ebil. Then -ir. Then the person. The blocks drop from above and lock together.

Root. Look at the last vowel.

After a vowel, add y. okuyabilirsin you can read
t before a vowel becomes d. gidebilirsin you can go

Put together: stroll, tail, you. What do you get?

Red flag — not allowed

A ban is -emez. Beach signs only talk like this. Tap — the sign swings and speaks.

Everyday requests. Grab them and use them. The answer to “can I?” sounds the same, just without the question.

yellow flag

Maybe

Now the tail talks about the world. Weather, the bus, the ferry. It promises nothing. It just warns.

Not sure — -ebilir. You see it with your own eyes — a regular tense.

The most useful word of the lesson. Maybe. They say it’ll be forty degrees tomorrow. You say olabilir. They ask if you got everything. You say olabilir.

There’s doubt with a minus too. “Might not come” — gelmeyebilir. Same tail, just with the negation in front.

You’re not sure the museum is open. How do you say it?

blue flag

My advice

A friend is in Izmir for the first time. Asks where to go. You don’t give orders. You say: you could go there. That’s advice. Like an order, but in shorts.

You can advise everyone at once: -ebilirsiniz. That’s how a tour guide on a bus talks. Polite, no pressure.

Your friend is tired after the beach. Suggest going to bed early.

English vs Turkish

Three words or one tail

In English the three meanings take three different words. In Turkish — one tail. The end of the phrase does the rest. Scroll down: the words merge into one block.

maymightcould -ebil

English “can” is split too: ability and permission. The Turkish tail also takes “might” and “I’d suggest.”

“It might snow tonight.” Which word translates “might”?

finale

Çeşme again. Same tower. Same lifeguard. You walk up. You say: Yüzebilir miyim?

He says: Yüzebilirsin. Ama rüzgâr çıkabilir. You nod. And this time you get all of it. Permission. A warning. You even answer: Olabilir.

Now you can ask, give permission, guess the weather and give advice. With one tail.

The wind never picked up, by the way.
