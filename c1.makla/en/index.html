01 · Viewing

Thirty-eight square meters

You're in Samsun. Viewing an apartment in Atakum.

The realtor says, daire deniz manzaralı olmakla beraber biraz küçük.

You hear “deniz manzaralı.” The middle of the sentence roars like the surf.

You say, I'll take it. He says, sadece söylemekle kalmayın, imzalayın da.

You sign. You move in. Thirty-eight square meters. The sea is visible. If you stand on a stool.

You missed that middle part. Olmakla beraber means “although.” The most expensive word in the contract.

The realtor didn't lie, by the way. He said everything. He just hid the important part in the middle.

04 · Although

I admitbut…

Beraber and birlikte don't mean “together” here. They turn the first part into a concession. Yes, it's true. But look what comes next.

Haklı olmakla beraber kibar davranmadı.

He was right — and was rude anyway.

The style is bookish, businesslike. In conversation people would rather say ama. The subject goes separately: -mAklA knows no person.

One more detail. The negation goes in the main part, not in -mAklA. Bilmekle beraber söylemedi — he knew but didn't say. The concession itself always sounds affirmative. Beraber and birlikte are interchangeable here: birlikte is a bit stricter, beraber a bit livelier.

A trap for the eye. With a noun, ile beraber is “together with.” With a -mAk verb it's “although.” Switch and compare.

05 · Not only

Not onlybut also

Kalmamak means “not to stop there.” The first floor is built, a second one goes on top. The second part almost always has da/de or bile.

Sadece söylemekle kalmadı, yaptı da.

He didn't just say it — he did it too.

Without the negation the meaning flips. Plan yapmakla kaldık — we never got past the plan. The second floor stayed a dotted line.

At C1 you'll also meet -DIğIylA kalmak, “to be left with only…” Sadece verdiği sözle kaldık: all we were left with was his promise.

Kalmamak conjugates like any verb: kalmadım, kalmayacağız, kalmaz. Any tense. And bile instead of da makes it stronger: not only… but even. You can drop sadece, but with it the thought sounds sharper.

07 · Compare

How English says it

English puts the concession in “although” and “even though,” and the bookish stuff in “notwithstanding.” And “not only… but also” is the twin of kalmadı.

English keeps the same split: concession on one side, addition on the other. Switch between casual and careful English and compare. To an English ear “notwithstanding” sounds like legalese. To a Turkish ear -mAklA beraber does a little too.

10 · How to remember

The makler always says “although”

It sounds like “Makler,” German for broker. A makler is a realtor. He always opens with a concession: manzaralı olmakla beraber…

And then he doesn't stop: göstermekle kalmaz, satar da. He'll show it — and sell it.

Makler is a real word from the trade. Let him be your prompter on the exam.
