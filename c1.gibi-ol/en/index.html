Scene 02Two bricks

To say “almost,” Turkish takes two bricks. You know both.

The first is gibi, “like, as if.” Teleferik kuş gibi. The cable car is like a bird.

The second is ol-. A porter verb: it carries no meaning of its own, but it hauls tense and person. Bitirmiş olacağım — I’ll have finished.

Glue them together: düşecek + gibi + oldum. The whole topic fits on one boarding pass.

Build the boarding pass yourself: tap the bricks in order.

Scene 03Almost

Guess first. Telefonum denize düşecek gibi oldu. Did the phone fall or not?

It didn’t. -ecek looks ahead: the fall was already on its way. And gibi oldu says: there was a moment, and it passed.

The rule in one line: verb + -(y)AcAk + gibi oldu = almost.

Otobüsü kaçıracak gibi oldum. I almost missed the bus.

Fiyatı duyunca bayılacak gibi oldum. I heard the price and almost fainted. (It was a fridge magnet.)

Drag the phone toward the sea. Stop short of the end — that’s gibi oldu.

Telefonum denize düşecek gibi oldu.The phone almost fell into the sea.

Scene 04As if already

The Bandırma steamship museum. The guide talks so fast, it’s like he’s late for this very ship.

Mira nods: Anladım! The guide: O zaman anlatın. Then tell us. Silence.

The honest answer would be: Anlamış gibi oldum. I thought I understood.

Rule: verb + -mIş + gibi oldu = as if it already happened. There’s a feeling, no guarantee.

Çayı içince ısınmış gibi oldum. I drank the tea and sort of warmed up.

The -mIş ending changes with the last vowel. Pick a verb, then the ending.

The episode’s cheat sheet on one screen. Three endings before gibi, one porter after.

-(y)AcAk gibi oldu

Almost: the event was on its way but never landed.
