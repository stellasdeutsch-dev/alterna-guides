She’s been learning Turkish for eight months. Just moved to Izmir.

She needs a pool. She doesn’t know the word for “pool.” But she knows yüzmek, to swim, and yer, place.

Receptiona hotel in Alsancak

She walks up to the desk. Says: Yüzmek yer nerede?

The receptionist looks at her. Then out the window. Then at her again.

Points at the sea. Deniz orada. The sea is over there.

Receptiona hotel in Alsancak

She should have said: Yüzme havuzu nerede? Where’s the pool.

01 What it is

Three formsof one verb

Take okumak, to read. A Turk makes three different words out of it. Each has its own job. Tap a card.

01 · -mak / -mek
Okumak
The action in general

This is how the verb sits in the dictionary. It works like a noun: you can want it, love it, ban it.

Kitap okumak güzel.Reading books is great.
02 · -ma / -me
Okuma
Name of an activity

A name for an action. It names rooms, pools, exams and even dishes.

Okuma salonu nerede?Where’s the reading room?
03 · -ış / -iş / -uş / -üş
Okuyuş
Manner and signs

Not the fact itself, but how a person does it. Plus ready-made words: entrance, exit, walk.

Onun okuyuşu çok güzel.He reads really beautifully.

In English these are different words: swim, pool, entrance, smile. In Turkish, each one has a verb with a tail behind it.
