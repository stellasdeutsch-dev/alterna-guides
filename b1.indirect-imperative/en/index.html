02. Transshipment

The order goes into a container

You can’t pass an order on as is. It gets repacked. Four bays, four steps. Tap the numbers or a bay.

English gets by with an infinitive: “told me to come.” Turkish can’t do that. It needs a noun: “my coming.”

And the accusative -i is there because the coming is the object here. Said what? My coming.

gelmemi söyledi

Literally: “said my coming.” That’s exactly how it sounds in Turkish.

03. Who got the order

Six addressees

Only the third bay changes. Whoever was told owns the coming. Switch the verb and tap a card.

If the stem has a, ı, o, u, you get -ma. If e, i, ö, ü, you get -me. Harmony as usual.

You know these endings. They’re the same “my, your, his” as in “my house, your house.” Just stuck onto the coming.

04. Route

Who speaks, who does it

There are two people in the sentence. The speaker hides at the end, in söyledi. The doer hides in the middle, in the tail. Move them separately.

The order never changes: who said it, to whom, the package, the verb.

Speaks:
Does:

05. Tone

Told, asked, asked very nicely

The verb at the end sets the tone. And the case. With söyledi we say bana. With istedi and rica etti we say benden: you ask “from me.”

A politeness scale. Not scientific. Tap a bar.

The middle stays put. Same package; only the final verb and the addressee’s case change.

Rica etmek means “to ask politely.” That’s how you write in emails and talk to your boss.

Bana gelmemi söyledi.

Told me to come.

06. Quotes

Direct or reported

You can quote. Then the order stays as is, in quotes, with dedi or diye next to it. Or you can report. Then it’s the package and söyledi.

Quotes love lively storytelling and gossip. At work and in emails people usually report with the package.

09. Traps

Four traps

You fell into the first one in the thread. Everyone trips on it.

The infinitive habit

gel söyledi → gelmemi söyledi

There’s never a bare order in the middle. Only the package.

He, she

gelmenı → gelmesini

After a vowel, “his” picks up an s, then an n: -sını, -sini.

Double “not”

«Gitme!» → gitmememi söyledi

The first -me is “not.” The second makes the coming. Sounds funny, and that’s right.

dedi or söyledi

gelmemi dedi → gelmemi söyledi

dedi loves quotes. The package rides with söyledi.
