That's mistake number one.

KENken.taksi

The driver asks where your Turkish is from. You say you studied. He says, when.

You say, ben öğrenci… was… when…

He says, öğrenciyken? You say yes. That.

KENken.taksi

The driver's thrilled. Küçükken this. Askerdeyken that.

You nod. You nod very confidently.

While you were nodding, he drove past your hotel.

Annoying. But he explained the grammar better than the textbook. Ken glues two events together.

02 The gist

Two events. One time.

One action is long. The other happens inside it.

The long one gets -ken. That's it.

In English that's “when,” “while,” or “as.” In Turkish — one ending on the long action.

04 Character

Ken is stubborn

Almost every Turkish suffix adapts. -de becomes -da, -ler becomes -lar.

Ken doesn't become anything. Like a building doorman: doesn't care who you are.

07 Person and tense

No person. No tense.

Ken doesn't know who you are. Ken doesn't know when.

The main verb knows. Ken just stands next to it. Stands very confidently.
