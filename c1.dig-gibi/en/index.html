( 03 · form )#ticket_printing

A ticket in four steps

Take the verb stem: de-. Add -DIk: dedik. It's not the past tense yet. It's a blank ticket.

Next, the owner: -in, “you.” The k between vowels softens to ğ: dediğin. Add gibi at the end — and the ticket is ready.

( step 1 )№ 002
dedikingibi
DE·DİK

The vowel in -DIk follows vowel harmony: dik, dık, duk, dük. After a voiceless consonant, d becomes t: yaptığı, anlattığı.

( 06 · owner )#whose_ticket

Whose ticket

The owner sits in the tail. You can add a pronoun if you want to stress it: senin dediğin gibi.

If the owner is a name, it goes into the genitive. And the tail is third person: Ali'nin dediği gibi, herkesin bildiği gibi.

With “they,” k doesn't soften: there's no vowel before -leri. dedikleri.

( 07 · meaning 1 )#as_is_known

Meaning 1. As is known

It's an introductory phrase. It says where you got the news from. It comes first, followed by a comma.

( 08 · meaning 2 )#the_way

Meaning 2. The way

It's a cover. The original is in the part with gibi. Your action comes after it.

( 10 · future )#as_you_will_see

A future ticket: -(y)AcAğI gibi

Is the event still ahead? Use -(y)AcAk. The owner and gibi stay the same: göreceğiniz gibi — “as you will see.”

Tour guides and lecturers love this one. The audience hasn't seen it yet but already knows they will.

( 11 · false bottom )#as_soon_as

False bottom: as soon as

Sometimes gibi doesn't compare anything. Two actions follow each other with no pause. Then -DIğI gibi means “as soon as.”

Eve geldiği gibi uyudu. He came home and fell asleep right away. Same as gelir gelmez.

eve geldiuyudu

The test is simple: is there anything to compare? No — then it's “as soon as.”

“Bileti aldığı gibi koştu” is…
