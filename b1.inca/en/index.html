1The note

Antalya, the old town of Kaleiçi. Oranges, heat, and tourists with maps.

Timur rents an apartment here. The landlady, Ayşe Teyze, went to visit her sister. She left him her dog Simit and a note on the fridge.

Yağmur başlayınca pencereleri kapat. Yağmur — rain. Pencere — window. Kapat — close. And the little tail -yınca Timur decided to ignore.

He closed all the windows. Right away. It's +38 outside. Simit is lying on the tiles, looking at Timur like an enemy of the people.

2Two shots

Cut the word from the note: başla — to begin, y — buffer, ınca — splice.

-(y)IncA splices two shots. The first — something happened. The second — what comes right after it.

In English it's “as soon as” or “when” — a separate word in front. Turkish has no such word: the tail sits at the end of the verb. And the -IncA shot always comes first.

Eve gelince seni ararım. As soon as I get home, I'll call you. Home first, then the call.

Haberi duyunca çok sevindi. Which shot came first?

3Signs

Timur and Simit are walking through Kaleiçi. There are four signs on the street. Guess first: dur — which one will it go to?

The splice vowel looks at the verb's last vowel. a and ı → -ınca: kalkınca. The ı is like the a in “about.”

e and i → -ince: gelince, said “geh-lin-jeh”: c = j in “jam.” Never k, never s.

o and u → -unca: durunca. Guessed it? Then harmony is already in your ears.

ö and ü → -ünce: görünce. English has no vowel harmony, so this is the part to drill.

Once the bus stops, get off. As soon as Simit saw me, he wagged his tail. The tail isn't grammar, but it's nice.

4The leash

Morning, the waterfront. Timur is walking Simit and learning the verb oku — to read.

oku + unca. Two vowels collided head-on. Turkish won't stand for that.

A y steps in between them, like the leash between Timur and Simit. It's an old rule: su → suyu. English does it too: a apple → an apple.

If the verb ends in a vowel — first y, then the splice. Same harmony.

Simit uyuyunca Timur da uyudu. Simit fell asleep — and so did Timur.

5Pazar

Morning, the pazar — the market. Watermelons the size of Simit. You can smell the oranges down the whole street.

Ayşe Teyze texts: Pazara gidince karpuz al. Timur looks up the verb gid in the dictionary. There's no such thing.

It's git — to go. Before a vowel the t softened and became d: gidince.

git, et and tat do this. The other verbs in ‑t hold on: kapatınca, bitince. Like go → went: just learn them.

6Second shot

Timur texts the landlady: Eve gelince seni ararım. As soon as I get home, I'll call.

The splice has no tense and no person. English half agrees: “when I get home,” not “when I will get.” Turkish goes all the way: gelince fits I get, she gets, we got.

The second shot carries the tense. ararım — aorist, a promise: “I'll call, my word.” aradım — already called. ara — call!

Who came is shown by the word next to it. Sen gelince Simit çok sevinir. When you come, Simit is really happy.

Switch the second shot:

As soon as I get home, I'll call you. A promise.

7The shoe

Timur stayed late at the beach. The sun set. Nobody took Simit out.

The apartment is quiet. Too quiet. In the middle of the rug lies what used to be a shoe.

The negation -mA goes before the splice, and after it comes a vowel again, so y: gel-me-y-ince. “Since he didn't come.” English needs “didn't”; Turkish hides the “not” inside the word.

The splice often sounds like a reason. Haberi duyunca çok sevindi. He heard the news — and that's why he was happy.

Switch the first shot:

Timur didn't come — Simit ate the shoe.

    Bad takes

    Cut, bad take

    Four mistakes an English speaker makes here. Tap “Reshoot” to see the clean take.

    Take 1

    Ne zaman eve gelirim, ararım.

    English “when” turned into a separate word. But ne zaman is only the question “when?”.

    Clean take

    Eve gelince ararım.

    No “when” word. One tail on the verb does the job.

    Take 2

    gelince = “geh-lin-keh”

    Read like English: c as k (or as s, like in “nice”). In Turkish c is always the j in “jam.”

    Clean take

    gelince = “geh-lin-jeh”

    And kalkınca: ı like the a in “about.”

    Take 3

    Simit beni görince sevindi.

    -ince picked as “the” form. But gör has ö: the lips stay round.

    Clean take

    Simit beni görünce sevindi.

    Four signs, not one: -ınca, -ince, -unca, -ünce.

    Take 4

    Kitabı okunca anladım.

    Forgot the leash: the verb ends in a vowel. Same reason as “an apple.”

    Clean take

    Kitabı okuyunca anladım.

    As soon as I read the book, I got it.
