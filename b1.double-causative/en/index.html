01

You’re in Antalya. Renting an apartment. The faucet drips. At night it drips louder. You count drops instead of sheep.

02

You call the landlord. Tell him the faucet’s leaking. He says: Tamam, hallederim. OK, I’ll handle it. You believe him. Mistake number one.

03

The landlord calls the property manager. He doesn’t go near the faucet. He’s in Izmir, actually.

04

The manager calls a plumber. The plumber shows up. Three days later. He knows nothing about the landlord. He knows about the faucet.

05

In the evening the landlord texts: Musluğu tamir ettirttim. I had the faucet fixed. He never touched it. But grammatically he’s right.

Two suffixes — two middlemen. The landlord told the manager, the manager told the plumber. That’s the whole lesson.

Every new suffix is a new floor. And a new person in the chain.

Careful with roots. piş has no people on the ground floor, so on the first floor the cook does the cooking himself. yap already has a person downstairs: yaptım — I did it myself. Then the first floor is a handyman, and the second is one more middleman.

003 · suffixes

Ping-pong -dIr ↔ -t

yap

After -dIr, -Ir and -Ar, -t hits back. After -t, -dIr hits back. Nobody hits twice from the same side. Hit, return, done.

yap → yaptır → yaptırt
öl → öldür → öldürt
temizle → temizlet → temizlettir
piş → pişir → pişirt

You know the first hit from the last topic. A short root ending in a consonant takes -dIr. A long one ending in a vowel, r, or l takes -t. Some roots take -Ir or -Ar. After that the ball just flies to the other side.

-dIr has eight variants: harmony and voicing. After t it’s voiceless: temizlettir, okuttur.

004 · cases

Who to whom

Patronwho orders raporuwhat · -u asistanawho does it · -a yazdırttı2 floors

The boss had the assistant write the report, through the manager. The last doer gets -(y)A.

Who orders — no ending. What gets done — with -(y)I. Who does it in the end — with -(y)A. The report flies across half the country, and the sentence is just four words.

The one in the middle usually isn’t named. He’s in the verb, but not in the cases. If you really need him, mention him in a separate sentence.

In the textbook — two middlemen. At the market in Antalya — more often just “not me personally.” Both meanings are correct.
