Barely landed — already calling

The plane touches the runway. The wheels are still rolling. And you're already calling Mom.

In Turkish it's one verb, clicked twice. Plus, minus. Zero seconds in between.

English needs three words for this: “as soon as.” Turkish needs none. Today: how to click, which tail to use, and why the pair never conjugates.

Hook

1.0Calling Mom

The airport. Mom is seeing you off to Antalya. She hugs you like you're flying to Mars.

She says, Uçak iner inmez ara!

You nod. You think: I'll call from the hotel. Calmly, with a sea view. When do you call?

You call from the hotel three hours later. Mom says, Üç saat oldu!

In three hours she called the airport, Aunt Gül and Aunt Gül's neighbor. The neighbor has already bought a ticket.

Inmez doesn't mean “sometime.” It means “the wheels are still rolling.”

You call while the plane is taxiing. Mom says, Aferin, hemen aradın!

The flight attendant glares. Mom is happy. Aunt Gül's neighbor stays home.

Rule

3.0Double click

Take the verb gel-, to come. Click it twice. Watch what each click does.

  1. gel- — the verb. Just “come.”
  2. gelir — first click, plus. The aorist.
  3. gelmez — second click, minus. Same verb.
  4. gelir gelmez + the main action. “As soon as I came…”

The minus here isn't negation. It's the click of a stopwatch: the first thing hasn't cooled down, and the second is already going.

  • Eve gelir gelmez uyudum.As soon as I got home, I fell asleep.
  • Sınav biter bitmez tatile çıkacağız.As soon as exams are over, we're going on vacation.
  • Beni görür görmez el salladı.As soon as he saw me, he waved.

First click

4.0Which tail goes on the plus

The first click is the aorist. You pick the tail by the old rules. The second click is easier: always -maz or -mez.

Better learn the thirteen exceptions as a list: al, bil, bul, dur, gel, gör, kal, ol, öl, san, var, ver, vur. Sound tip: ı is the a in “about,” so alır ≈ “ah-luhr.” Tap a verb. The dot will carry it to the right lane.

5.0The pair doesn't conjugate

The pair doesn't know who came. It's frozen. English says “as soon as I got home, I…” — Turkish says “I” once, at the very end. Tap an avatar.

6.0Zero seconds

You already know -ince: “when.” An hour, lunch and half a TV series can pass between the events.

-ir -mez can't stand a pause. Scroll or drag the slider: the bars slide together and the form changes by itself. Half an hour's pause is bitince. Zero is biter bitmez.

English ↔ Turkish

7.0English says “no” too

English has its own minus that denies nothing: “No sooner had I walked in than the phone rang.” There's a “no.” Nobody's saying no.

Everyday English just says “as soon as” or “the moment.” Turkish has no such word. It takes the verb itself: plus, then minus. Flip the switch.

No sooner had I walked in than the phone rang.
Eve girer girmez telefon çaldı.
As soon as I wake up, I drink coffee.
Uyanır uyanmaz kahve içerim.
The moment exams are over, we're leaving.
Sınav biter bitmez tatile çıkacağız.
