Turkish past: gelmiş — did you see it or hear it?

At first Turkish feels easy. Words stick to the root like beads. No articles, no irregular verbs.

Then comes the past tense. Turkish has two. Not “I went” vs “I have gone.” The difference is how you know: did you see it, or did you hear it?

Turkish class. The hoca asks: Dün ne yaptın?What did you do yesterday?
Student: Dün sinemaya gitmişim.meant to say “I went to the movies yesterday”
The hoca looks at him: Emin misin?Are you sure?
What he actually said: “Apparently I went to the movies yesterday.” Somebody told him.
The class cracks up. Student: “I just wanted it to sound fancier.”

It did sound fancier. As if someone else took him to the movies and he found out the next morning. From Instagram.

What if you answer differently?
-dıDün sinemaya gittim.I went to the movies. I was there. Ticket in my pocket.
vs
-mışDün sinemaya gitmişim.Apparently I went to the movies. Don't remember it. Someone told me.
01 / why two

Why Turks need two pasts

A Turkish verb always tells how you know. Tap a petal.

Eyes means -dı: I was there, I saw it, I did it. Ears, surprise and fairy tale mean -mış: I was told, I found out from the result, that's how it's retold.

English verbs have no such button. “He came” covers both. To show you only heard it, you add a word: apparently, they say, it turns out. Turkish puts that word inside the verb, as a tail. Geldi — he came. Gelmiş — apparently he came.

And this matters: -mış doesn't mean “not true.” It's not gossip in the bad sense. It's an honest label: I didn't see it myself.

03 / form

Build -mış piece by piece

Guess first. A mistake here costs nothing. oku + mış = ?

The form goes together like beads on a string: root, the -mış tail, the personal ending. Nothing changes inside the root.

The vowel in the tail follows the last vowel of the root. Four options. Tap a pair of vowels.

1

Four tails

miş, mış, muş, müş — by the last vowel of the root.

2

No devoicing

-dı can turn into t: baktı. With -mış the m always stays m: bakmış. One rule fewer.

3

Long endings

Like the present tense: -im, -sin, -iz, -siniz, -ler.

Say it right: ş = “sh.” miş sounds like “mish,” muş like a short “moosh.” In mış the ı is the a in “about.” For müş, say “mish” with rounded lips.

Tap a person to hear how it sounds. O has no ending.

English ↔ Turkish

English adds a word. Turkish adds a tail.

In English, “came” is just “came.” If you only heard it, you add a word in front: apparently, they say, turns out. Turkish packs that word into the verb.

    The whole topic on one screen

    miş · mış · muş · müş — by the last vowel of the root. No devoicing: bakmış.

    -mış — I was told, or turns out. -dı — I saw it, I know for sure.

    Not sure — ask the questions in order. Did I see it myself? Yes — -dı. Was I told? Yes — -mış. Did I find out from the result and get surprised? Yes — -mış. In short: eyes — -dı, ears — -mış.

    The hoca won't ask “emin misin?” anymore. Now you know how you know.
