How we get results

The root. Then -ş: “with each other.” Then -tır: “make them do it.” Each suffix adds one more person to the story.

What can you sew onto -ş?

After ş it's always t: a voiceless letter won't let d anywhere near it. The vowel in the suffix follows the last vowel of the root. Four outfits: -tır, -tir, -tur, -tür. The vowel adapts, the consonant never does. The letter t is as stubborn as the tax office.

English swaps the word, Turkish adds a suffix

Get acquainted → introduce: English grabs a whole new verb. Turkish just sews on -tır. One more suffix — one more person in the story. English hides the go-between in a different word. Turkish writes it right into the verb.

get acquainted → introducetanış → tanıştır
make up → reconcilebarış → barıştır
meet up → bring togetherbuluş → buluştur
agree → broker a dealanlaş → anlaştır

Don't confuse it with tanıtmak

tanıtmak — to present: yourself, a city, a product. One person shows, everyone else watches.

tanıştırmak — to bring two people together so they get to know each other. Without ş there's no “each other.”

Tell your mom “seni Mert'le tanıttım” and it'll sound like you were advertising Mert.

Remember the matchmaker

-ş is the two at the table. -tır is the matchmaker who sat them there. In life, dates happen without a matchmaker. In grammar too: the suffix is just shorter. The matchmaker is always the third one. Even if it's your mom.

Without -tır you're the one meeting someone. The go-between is gone.

03

Who gets introduced — accusative, not dative.

04
Bizi buluştırdı.

The suffix vowel listens to the root: u means -tur.

05

After ş only t. The letter d doesn't come here.

06
Ben onları barıştım.

Make up yourself — barıştım. Reconcile others — barıştırdım.
