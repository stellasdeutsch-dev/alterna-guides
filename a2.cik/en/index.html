    03 · exception

    K has left the chat

    Lena’s mistake was hiding at the very end of the word.

    küçü—k

    küçükcük

    k has left the chat

    ufakcıkitty-bitty
    sıcakcıknice and hot
    yumuşakcıksoft and squishy

    Lena opens her laptop. She’s looking for where she tripped.

    If an adjective ends in k, that k drops before the suffix.

    küçük loses its tail, leaving küçü. Plus cük. You get küçücük

    ufak, sıcak, yumuşak work the same way. The tail goes, the suffix moves in.

    04 · voiceless

    The pistachio seller switches on ç

    Mert sends a voice message with the trick Turks learn in school.

    fıstıkçı

    Fıstıkçı Şahap

    “Şahap the pistachio seller.” The green ones are all the voiceless consonants.

    kitapçıkbooklet
    kuşçukbirdie
    köpekçikdoggie
    azıcıka tiny bit
    daracıknice and narrow

    Mert apologizes for laughing and sends a tip.

    All the voiceless consonants are hiding in this phrase: f, s, t, k, ç, ş, h, p.

    After them, c goes voiceless too: ç, like ch in church.

    The noun köpek keeps its tail. Only adjectives lose the k.

    And short little az and dar ask for an extra vowel.

    05 · meaning

    Cute or stronger

    The same tail does different jobs.

    mini

    Thing → cute

    • kedicik — kitty
    • kuzucuk — little lamb

    Quality → stronger

    • küçücük — teeny-tiny
    • sıcacık — nice and hot

    kitapçık

    not a “little book,” a booklet

    In the evening Mert explains why Turks even need this suffix.

    With things and animals, -CIk makes them cute. Like English -y and -ie: kitty, doggie.

    With a quality, it intensifies: not just small, but teeny-tiny. English needs two words. Turkish needs one suffix.

    And kitapçık has long lived on its own. It’s a booklet, not a cute little book. Same as English -let.

    06 · English ↔ Turkish

    English ↔ Turkish

    What’s the same takes one line. What’s different takes three cards. Open a card.

    ✓An ending, like English -ie and -letkitty — kedicikbooklet — kitapçıkc = j in jam · ç = ch in church

    01

    One ending, four shapes

    English

    kittydoggiebirdie

    Turkish

    kedicikkısacıkkuzucukküçücük

    English -ie looks the same on every word. Turkish copies the word’s last vowel: a, ı → cık, e, i → cik, o, u → cuk, ö, ü → cük.

    02

    Turkish writes the sound change

    English

    dogs [z]cats [s]

    Turkish

    kedicikkitapçıkkuşçuk

    In English the s in “cats” sounds different from “dogs”, but the letter stays. Turkish changes the letter: after a voiceless sound, c becomes ç.

    03

    Teeny-tiny is one word

    English

    teeny-tinynice and hot

    Turkish

    küçücüksıcacıkazıcık

    English adds a word to make it stronger. Turkish adds -cIk to the adjective. One suffix does the job of the extra English word: küçücük = teeny-tiny.

    08 · bug reports

    Bugs English speakers hit

    küçükcük

    küçücük

    English just adds -ie to the whole word. Turkish adjectives drop the final k first.

    kuzucik

    kuzucuk

    No vowel harmony in English, so one form feels safe. It isn’t: last vowel u → -cuk.

    kitapcık

    kitapçık

    English hides this change in sound: cats [s], dogs [z]. Turkish writes it: p is voiceless → ç.

    annecikim

    anneciğim

    Mommy dearest. Before a vowel, k becomes ğ. It’s silent and just stretches the vowel: “an-neh-jeem”.

    The -cIk form

    • a, ı → cık · e, i → cik
    • o, u → cuk · ö, ü → cük

    k drops

    • in adjectives: küçük → küçücük
    • ufacık, sıcacık, yumuşacık

    Voiceless → ç

    • fıstıkçı Şahap: kitapçık, kuşçuk
    • + a vowel: azıcık, daracık

    Meaning

    • thing — cute: kedicik
    • quality — stronger: küçücük

    Now you put -CIk in four forms, drop the k, and switch on ç.
