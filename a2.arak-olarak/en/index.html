Doing, by doing

koşarak olarak

Last topic you were cutting actions together: kalkıp çıktı — got up and left. Today the editing gets trickier.

-arak lays one action over another: koşarak geldi — came running. And olarak puts the role in the credits: öğretmen olarak — as a teacher.

Three meanings, one form, and one word for roles. Action!

Scene 1form

How -arak is built

Four steps, like a clapperboard: scene, take, action, cut. One tail for every person — -arak doesn't know who's acting.

okuyarak
  1. Take the verb stem: okumak without -mak — oku.
  2. Look at the last vowel: u, back.
  3. Back a ı o u — tail -arak. Front e i ö ü — -erek.
  4. Stem ends in a vowel? Add y. That's a wrap: okuyarak.

Two special actors: ye and de change e to i before y: yiyerek, diyerek. And the t in git softens: giderek.

Scene 2the “how?” meaning

The big question: how?

-arak answers the question “how?” How did he come? Koşarak — running. How did she answer? Gülerek — laughing.

English often uses an adverb or a phrase here: on foot, in tears, out loud. Turkish uses a verb with a tail. Left — crying, answered — smiling.

Change the manner and watch the shot change.

Scene 3at the same time

Two tracks at once

Second meaning: two actions happen at the same time. The main action is the video in the shot. The -arak action is the audio track underneath.

Müzik dinleyerek ders çalışıyorum: studying in the shot, music in the background. Hit “play” and watch the tracks.

The golden rule of editing: the -arak track can't live without video. Without the main verb the sentence isn't finished.

Scene 4means

Means: “by doing”

Third meaning — the means. How did you get the result? Spor yaparak kilo verdim — I lost weight by working out.

Hence the topic's title: “doing” and “by doing.” Means on the left, result on the right. Tap — the arrow shows the link.

English says “by doing” or “through.” Turkish gets by with the same -arak.

Scene 5-ıp and -arak

Cut or overlay

Last topic had -ıp: first one thing, then the other. Kalkıp çıktı — got up and left. That's a cut: clip, scissors, next clip.

-arak is an overlay: both clips play at once. Koşarak çıktı — ran out. Switch and watch how the tracks behave.

Meaning check: can you add “then”? Then it's the -ıp cut.

Scene 6who's acting

The person goes at the end

-arak doesn't carry a person. The main verb at the end tells who's doing it. Both actions are done by the same person.

Gülerek konuştum — I talked, laughing. Gülerek konuştular — they did. The -erek tail didn't budge. Handy: learn the form once, it fits everyone.

Scene 7negation

Without doing: -mayarak

Need “without doing”? Put -ma or -me before the tail. A y goes between them: -mayarak, -meyerek.

Annesini dinlemeyerek hata yaptı — not listening to his mom, he made a mistake. The red block is the “not.”

The order is the usual, left to right: stem, negation, y, tail.

Scene 8role in the credits

olarak: as

olarak is a separate word after a noun. It means “as,” “in the role of.” Öğretmen olarak çalışıyorum — I work as a teacher.

There are set phrases too: ilk olarak — first of all, genel olarak — in general. Check out this lesson's credits.

The key thing: olarak doesn't stick to the word. It's written separately, like the role under a name in the credits.

Memory trickprojector sound

Ta-ra-rak

Listen to an old film projector: ta-ra-rak, ta-ra-rak. The reel keeps spinning while the main action plays on screen.

Same with -arak: it clatters in the background while the main verb is in the shot. Turn off the projector — -arak is over too. Repeat it three times to the clatter, and the tail will stick on its own.

The vowel ü is front. Front vowels get -erek.

The stem ends in a vowel. Without y two vowels trip over each other.
