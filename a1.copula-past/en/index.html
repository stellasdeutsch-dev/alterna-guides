Meaning / rule 1

Today → yesterday

Today: hastayım — I am sick. Yesterday: hastaydım — I was sick.

The word “yesterday” (dün) is useful, but it doesn’t change the form. The tail does.

The difference is one little piece: -dı. English adds a separate word, “was.” Turkish glues a tail onto the word itself.

No verb. No gender. No extra words. Works with adjectives, nouns and places: hastaydım, öğrenciydim, evdeydim.

How it works / rule 2

Four steps of assembly

Word, y bridge, -dı and the person tail. Transparent terms, like a good company.

The y bridge is needed only if the word ends in a vowel: hasta-y-dı-m. After a consonant you don’t need it: yorgun-du-m.

Harmony / rule 3

The wheel of eight tails

The -dı tail changes its vowel by harmony: dı, di, du, dü.

After the voiceless f, s, t, k, ç, ş, h, p you write t instead of d: açtım, küçüktü, sıcaktı.

These are the same rules as for verbs in the past. Only one thing is new — the y bridge after a vowel.

Tap a word — the wheel turns to its tail.

Persons / rule 4

Who was?

Person tails — same as verbs in the past: -m, -n, nothing, -k, -nız, -lar.

Ben hastaydım, biz hastaydık. No gender: hastaydı works for he, she and it.

The person tail goes at the very end: nothing comes after it.

For “they” the -lar is often dropped: onlar evdeydi. Not a mistake, just casual laziness.

Negation / rule 5

Wasn’t: değildim

“Wasn’t” is değil plus the same tail.

Değil doesn’t change, like a rock. You just glue -di and the person onto it: değildim, değildin, değildi.

Değildim means “I wasn’t.” Değil, di and m: three parts, no surprises.

No y bridge: değil ends in a consonant. And no harmony either — değil always takes -di.

Question / rule 6

Were you? mıydın?

A question is built with the particle mı.

The particle goes after the word and takes the past and person tails for itself: hasta mıydın? Not hastaydın mı.

The particle follows harmony too: hasta mıydın, evde miydin, yorgun muydun, üzgün müydün.

The particle is written separately, after a space. Everything after it is one word.

Comparison / English, Turkish, Kazakh

No gender and no “was”

English needs a separate word: was, were. Turkish doesn’t care who you are — and doesn’t need the word.

A man and a woman both say hastaydım. And Kazakh is a close relative again: üyde edim — evdeydim. Turkish -ydı is the old word idi, squeezed.

Traps / six of them

Six traps. Everyone falls in.

English whispers in your ear. Tap “fix” — and watch it get repaired.

01 · Bare word
Dün ben hasta.
Dün hastaydım.I was sick yesterday.

Without the tail the phrase is about now. “Yesterday” alone changes nothing.

02 · Forgotten bridge
Hastadım.
Hastaydım.I was sick.

The word ends in a vowel — you need a y between the vowels.

03 · Voiceless letter
Dün çok açdım. Çay sıcakdı.
Dün çok açtım. Çay sıcaktı.I was really hungry yesterday. The tea was hot.

After ç and k — t. Otherwise your tongue trips.

04 · Negation
Evde değilydim.
Evde değildim.I wasn’t home.

Değil ends in a consonant. The y bridge is extra there.

05 · Question
Hastaydın mı?
Hasta mıydın?Were you sick?

The past and person tails live on the particle mı, not on the word.

06 · “It was” ≠ vardı
Dün hava soğuk vardı.
Dün hava soğuktu.It was cold yesterday.

Vardı means “there was, there existed.” “It was cold” is just soğuk plus the tail.
