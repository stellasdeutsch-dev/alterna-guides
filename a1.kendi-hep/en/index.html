03
rule 1 · myself

Six plates for kendi

kendi (say “ken-dee”) means “self.” But it never rides bare. It needs a possessive tail, the same one as in evim “my house”, evin “your house”.

English does it with a word in front: myself, yourself. Turkish does it with a tail at the end. kendi ends in a vowel, so the tails are short: -m, -n, -si, -miz, -niz, -leri.

Tap a scooter to hear its plate. Orange is the tail; the rest is shared by all.

04
rule 1 · why

“Myself” is emphasis

The sentence works without kendim. But with it, you're banging your fist on the table: nobody helped. One English habit to drop: “I do it myself” puts it at the end. Turkish puts it before the verb. You can drop ben. The plate stays.

Ali kendisi geliyor.Ali is coming himself.
Biz kendimiz gidiyoruz.We're going ourselves.
Onlar kendileri geliyorlar.They're coming themselves.

The verb's tail and kendi's tail talk about the same person. They always match.

Are you doing it yourselves?

05
rule 2 · for myself

For myself: a boomerang with a plate

“For myself” is kendi with a plate plus the dative -e. The gift flies off and comes right back to you.

English needs two words: for myself. Turkish needs one: the “for” is the tail -e. Kendime çay alıyorum.I'm getting myself some tea.

Notice kendisine and kendilerine: an n slips in between the tail and -e. Like in ona.

Ayşe is buying herself a ticket.

06
rule 3 · my own

My very own

Before a noun, kendi takes off its plate. The tail moves onto the thing: kendi evim, my own house. In English the noun stays bare: own house. In Turkish it can't: evim.

The difference is like renting versus buying. benim evim — my house. kendi evim — my own house, nobody can take it.

07
rule 4 · all

Hep — the whole fleet at once

hep (rhymes with “step”) plus a tail means “all.” One word, no “of”. There are only three forms: hepimiz — all of us, hepiniz — all of you, hepsi — all of them, or everything.

The verb follows the tail. hepimiz drags “we” along: Hepimiz öğrenciyiz.We're all students.

For “they,” Turkish takes hepsi, not hepleri. And not hep onlar, word for word. That's fleet law.

Are you all ready?
