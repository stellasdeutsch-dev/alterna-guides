Scene · Kadıköy, a bookstore

Kitapı arıyorum. The clerk sighed

You walk into a bookstore and say kitapı arıyorum. I'm looking for the book. The clerk checks: kitabı? You nod: kitapı.
He silently brings the book: bu kitabı mı? You: evet, bu kitapı. You stare at each other. The clerk sighs.
By evening it all makes sense. The clerk wasn't mocking you. Before a vowel, p becomes b. He was speaking Turkish, and you — like a robot.

Today we figure out why p turns into b. And when it stays p after all.

Rule · What it is

A word is a building set

Turkish hooks suffixes onto the root like train cars. Watch what happens at the seam.

rootsuffixletter changed

The whole rule in one line. Voiceless p, ç, t, k at the end + a suffix starting with a vowel = voiced b, c, d, ğ. The letter got caught between vowels and went soft. Hence the name: yumuşama, softening.
1Writing. Say kitapı — people will understand. Write it — you lose a point on the exam.
2Dictionary. The text says dolabı. The dictionary has no dolab, only dolap.
3Listening. You hear gidiyor, but the textbook says gitmek. It's the same verb.

Rule · Verbs

The club of four

In verbs, only t softens. And only in four of them. Tap a stop.

In the club

  • gidiyorgitmek · is going
  • ediyoretmek · is doing
  • tadıyortatmak · is tasting
  • güdüyorgütmek · is herding

The rest hold on to t

  • satıyorsatmak · is selling
  • bitiyorbitmek · is ending
  • yatıyoryatmak · is lying down
You already say this. Teşekkür ederim — thank you. That's etmek: t became d, and you didn't even notice.

Like in English

English does it too. Just rarely

In Englishleafwe hear [f]leaves
In Turkishkitapwe write what we hearkitabı

English does this with a handful of words: leaf → leaves, knife → knives. Add an ending, and f turns into v right on paper. Turkish does it with almost every word ending in p: a vowel arrives, and it becomes b.

In Englishknifewe hear [f]knives
In Turkishçocukwe write what we hearçocuğu

Same with k. Only it turns not into g, but into the soft ğ.

Out loud, [kitabuh] comes easy. But your hand writes kitapı, because the root feels fixed. One thought fixes it: in Turkish, the letter follows the sound.
