Rule

Four blocks. One request

Examples first. Look at the tails.

Açar mısın?Will you open it?
Verir misin?Will you give it?
Bekler misin?Will you wait?

Noticed? The verb is in the aorist. Then the question. That’s it. Say it: açar mısın ≈ “ah-CHAR muh-suhn.” ç = ch in chair, ı = the a in about. The stress stays on -char: mı never takes it.

açarmısın

Person: sın — informal you. Formal you is mısınız.

The order never changes. Verb, tense, question, person. Like stops on a route: Konak doesn’t come after Alsancak.

05
Informal or formal

sen or siz: who gets which

English has one “you.” Turkish has two. Friends and siblings get sen. Drivers, sellers, doctors get siz — the “sir” or “ma’am” is built into the ending.

Erring on the side of siz is fine. Erring on the side of sen is awkward. Like hugging the cashier.

The particle listens to the verb’s last vowel. Four vowels — four particles. Same harmony as in the first city. English has nothing like it, so there is no “default” form: misin is not the safe choice.

07
English and Turkish

English asks with “will” and “could”

The idea is the same in both languages: a question is softer than an order. Open it! — Will you open it? Turkish does it too.

The tools are different. English needs a helper in front: can, will, could, would you mind. Turkish needs no helper. The verb goes into the aorist, and mı goes at the end: açar mısın?

10
Magic phrase

Bakar mısınız? to call the waiter

The waiter isn’t looking. You wave. He isn’t looking. You cough. He isn’t looking. You say, Bakar mısınız? He’s already at your table. With the menu.

Literally: will you look? That’s how you call a waiter, a seller, a passerby.

The answer is shorter than the request

Olur is the key word. It agrees to everything: tea, a discount, a wedding.

16
Traps

Five potholes on the route

The first four come straight from English. Nothing to be ashamed of. Walking two kilometers back from Konak — that’s embarrassing.

Açacak mısın?Açar mısın?

In English, “Will you open it?” is a request. Turkish -acak is the real future: “Are you going to open it someday?” Requests use the aorist.

Şoför Bey, açar mısın?Şoför Bey, açar mısınız?

English has one “you.” Turkish has two. A driver is a stranger: siz, mısınız.

Açarsın mı?Açar mısın?

You know açtın mı from the past tense: there “you” sits on the verb. Not here. In the aorist question, “you” rides on the particle: açar mı-sın.

Açar misin?Açar mısın?

No default vowel. The particle copies the verb’s last vowel: açar — a, so mı. ı is the a in about.

Alar mısın?Alır mısın?

al is an exception. Thirteen short verbs take -Ir: alır, gelir, verir.

Tense tail

Ends in a vowel — -r. One syllable — -Ar. More syllables and the thirteen exceptions — -Ir.

To whom

People you know — mısın. Strangers and elders — mısınız.

Don’t mix up

açacak mısın — plans, even if English says “will you.” açıyor musun — right now. açarsın mı — wrong order. The request — açar mısın.
