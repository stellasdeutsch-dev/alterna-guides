Any span of time or space in Turkish is built from three pieces. A start with the -DAn tail. A finish with the -(y)A tail. And the word kadar — “until.” Six minutes — and any schedule is yours.

Nine to nine

Istanbul. Kadıköy. You’ve decided to start a new life. On Monday, as one does.

You walk into a gym. You ask, what are your hours? The receptionist says, dokuzdan dokuza kadar. Nine to nine.

You ask, a.m. or p.m.? He nods. You show up at nine p.m. The lights go out. The guard says, kapalı.

The new life is moved to Monday. Next Monday.

tails

Eight tails

The tails change by two old rules. Vowel: a or e. Voicing: after a voiceless consonant, d becomes t. Tap to hear. You already know both rules, this is just review.

Voiceless: ç f h k p s ş t. Easy to remember with the phrase “fıstıkçı şahap.”

A city with a capital letter — the tail goes after an apostrophe: Kadıköy'den, Beşiktaş'a.

compare

Everything backwards

In English the little words go in front: “from” morning “till” evening.

In Turkish the tails stick on at the back, and kadar closes the phrase. Kazakh works exactly the same way.

Switch languages and watch where the little words go.
