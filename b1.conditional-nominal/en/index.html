Rule 1 · form

The -sa clip

Meet -sa. A little clip, like a capo on a guitar.

Clip it on the neck and the song plays in a new key. Clip it on a word and the word sounds like “if.”

yorgunsan
  1. Take a word: yorgun — tired.
  2. Look at the last vowel: u.
  3. The vowels a ı o u give sa, the vowels e i ö ü give se.
  4. Add the person: sen is n. Done: yorgunsan.

Rule 2 · the joint

Ends in a vowel? y first

Turkish doesn’t like two vowels in a row. hasta + sa stumbles like fingers on a first chord.

A y steps in between. A soft cushion, like a pick between finger and string.

Rule 3 · who

Six persons, one clip

After -sa comes the person. The endings are the same as in gelirsem, gelirsen. You already know them.

For “they,” people often just say yorgunsa. That there are many is clear from onlar.

Rule 4 · there is and there isn’t

There or not: varsa and yoksa

Meet the brothers var and yok. The first one has everything. The second one just shrugs.

With the clip they’re heard more than anything: on menus, at reception, in chats. Sorunuz varsa sorun. If you have questions, ask.

Boş odaa free hotel room
Zamanınyour time
Sorunuzyour question

Whose it is shows in the tail of the word: zamanın — your time, sorunuz — your (pl.) question. var has no person of its own.

“If you don’t have money…”

Rule 5 · negation

Not tired? The clip goes on değil

“Not” with a word is değil. The word stays put, and the clip jumps onto değil.

yorgundeğilsen

yorgunsan — if you’re tired

Yorgunsan dinlen.

In Turkish the verb “to be” hides. So “not to be” is a separate word, değil. The whole condition hangs on it.

“If you’re (pl.) not home…”

Comparison · word order

English ↔ Turkish

In English, “if” comes first as a separate word. In Turkish, the condition rides in the tail.

You can put the word eğer at the start for clarity. Or not. The clip is needed either way.

TRYorgunsandinlen.

4words in English → 2words in Turkish

Memory hook · three strings

A three-note chord

Three notes, always in the same order: y, clip, person.

  • yonly after a vowel
  • sa/sealways, by the last vowel
  • m n kperson, unless it’s “he”

Skip a note and the chord buzzes. Play them in order and it rings clean.

Traps · before and after

Where everyone trips

Everyone trips here. That’s fine: a trap you can see is no longer a trap.

hastasan
hastaysan

The word ends in a vowel. Without y the clip won’t hold.

evdesan
evdeysen

Two mistakes at once. Ends in a vowel — you need y. Vowel e — so se.

Eğer yorgunsun, dinlen.
Yorgunsan dinlen.

English habit: put “if” up front and you’re done. Nope. The clip is required, eğer is optional.

Ekmek değilse simit al.
Ekmek yoksa simit al.

No bread is yok. And değil means “it’s not bread.”

Paran varsan
Paran varsa

The person already sits in paran. You don’t add it twice: just varsa.
