As much as: -DIğI kadar -(y)AcAk kadar

A film in four scenes about a dinner where I ordered five pides without meaning to. Scroll down — the frame moves.

Scene 1 · Dinner in Atakum

Samsun, Atakum. My friend Deniz's aunt invites me to dinner. She texts me.

Akşam yemeğe gel, istediğin kadar kal!Come over for dinner, stay as long as you want! I got the word “come.” The rest is intuition.

The table. Pide. Aunt Nermin puts a third one on my plate. I want to say: “give me as many as you like, I don't mind.”

I say: Ne kadar istiyorsun? She says: Beş tane! And puts down five.How many do you want? — Five!

An hour later I want to say “I'm so full I can't get up.” I say: Çok doydum çünkü kalkamıyorum. “I'm full because I can't get up.” Deniz is crying with laughter.

I was two sentences short. Flip the cards — here they are.

Scene 2As much as you want

Scene 2 · As much as you want

I take apart what the aunt wrote. You've seen the tail -DIk before: istediğin — “what you want.”

The word kadar is familiar too: senin kadar — “as… as you, your height.” Guess what happens if you glue them together.

İstediğin kadar kal!Stay as long as you want! “What you want” plus “as much” — and the aunt isn't kicking me out.

-DIğI kadar — “as much as” or “as far as.” Who wants it shows in the ending: -im, -in, -i. Normal vowel harmony; after voiceless consonants — yaptığın.

Bildiğim kadar, Deniz yarın geliyor.As far as I know, Deniz is coming tomorrow. Elimden geldiği kadar yardım ederim.I'll help as much as I can.

Pick who wants it — the form rebuilds itself.

istediğin kadar

Scene 3So much that

Scene 3 · So much that

The second sentence. I want to say: “I'm so full I can't get up.” The fullness meter needle is in the red zone.

In English it's “full” first, then “that I can't get up.” In Turkish it's the other way around. Guess where kadar goes.

Kalkamayacak kadar doydum.I'm so full I can't get up. The result is on the left, the quality on the right, kadar in the middle.

-(y)AcAk kadar with no owner ending — “so… that” or “enough to.”

Ayakta duramayacak kadar yorgundu.He was so tired he couldn't stand. Herkese yetecek kadar yemek var.There's enough food for everyone.

Turn the needle: “We laughed so hard we cried.”

Scene 4Three plans

Scene 4 · Three plans

End of the evening. The aunt is packing food into containers: Yiyebileceğin kadar al!Take as much as you can eat!

Here it's future with an owner: yiyebileceğin — “what you'll be able to eat.” You haven't eaten it yet, but the plans are serious.

Me: Taşıyabileceğim kadar alırım.I'll take as much as I can carry. Deniz: Yediğin kadar da bulaşık yıkarsın.And you'll wash as many dishes as you ate.

Three plans. -DIğI kadar — how much there is or was. -(y)AcAğI kadar — how much there will be. -(y)AcAk kadar with no owner — “so… that.”

Pick a plan: “Pay for as much as you ate.”
