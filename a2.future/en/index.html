The future tense isn't about time. It's about the seam

You learn the suffix in an evening. The seams take a month. We'll go through all of them now, and you won't need the month.

Rule 1

Stem + suffix + person

Cut off -mek/-mak — the stem is left. Glue on the future suffix. Personal ending on top. The order never changes.

stemfuturewho does it

Like English “I'll come”: the pieces go in order. Only in Turkish it's one word, the seams show, and each piece has its own job.

Which of the two

-ecek or -acak

The suffix is tailored to the word. The measurement comes from the last vowel of the stem. Tap any vowel.

e-ecek

e, i, ö, ü → -ecek · a, ı, o, u → -acak

gelecek to come: last vowel e
yazacak to write: last vowel a
görecek to see: ö is front too
okuyacak to read: u is back, plus a buffer

Rule 2 Three seams

Where everyone breaks

You already know the suffix. Next come three spots where letters change on their own. Tap “Stitch” and watch.

k → ğ

a vowel follows: only ben and biz
gelecekim

In sen, o, siz, onlar a consonant follows — k stays calm: geleceksin.

buffer y

the stem ends in a vowel
bekle·ecek

Turkish can't stand two vowels in a row. A guard steps in between them: arayacak.

t → d

short stems ending in t
gitecek

The stem itself changes. There aren't many of these verbs, but they're common: git → gidecek, et → edecek.

All three share one logic: it's awkward for Turkish.

Holding k between vowels is awkward — it softens to ğ. Putting vowels side by side is awkward — y squeezes in. A voiceless t before a vowel is awkward — it becomes a voiced d.

One table and a switch

Pink rows are where k becomes ğ. Switch the verb: every e becomes a, every i becomes ı. Nothing else changes.

You're not learning two tables. You're learning one plus a switch. Tap a row to hear the form.
