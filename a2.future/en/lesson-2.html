The future talks

You're in Izmir texting a friend: “Will you come tomorrow?” English flips the words: will you. Turkish never flips. Your hand types whatever seems logical.

geleceksinmi?

Watch where the ending moves.

The ending doesn't stay put. In a question it moves onto the particle. The negative, on the other hand, crawls inside the word. Today — both moves and how the future sounds on the street.

Rule 3

The negative goes inside the word

The particle -me/-ma sits between the stem and the suffix. Right after it — buffer y: no two vowels in a row. The order: stem, negative, buffer, suffix, person.

English adds a separate word: will not, won't. Turkish has no “not” word for verbs. The no goes inside: gel-me-y-eceğim.

gelmeyeceğim I won't come
yazmayacağız we won't write
gitmeyeceğim I won't go: m comes after git, nothing to voice

gelecek değilim is correct too, but the shade is different: “I'm not planning to come.” For a plain “I won't come,” use -me.

Rule 4

Questions: mi takes the ending

English asks by flipping: you will → will you. Turkish keeps the order and adds mi, written separately. The personal ending moves onto it. The verb keeps a bare gelecek.

Gelecek misin?

Will you come?

Yazacak mıyız? Will we write? Back vowel — mı.
Gelmeyecek misin? Aren't you coming? Negative and question together.

The logic is simple: mi is needed where the answer is “yes” or “no.”

The exception is onlar: -ler stays on the verb, the particle goes bare.

There's a question word — no mi needed. The word already asks the question.

Gelecek misin?

Will you come?

Write it long. Hear it short

One more thing. For something close and settled, Turks often use the present tense with -yor. Like English “I'm flying tomorrow.”

Yarın İstanbul'a gideceğim.

I'll go to Istanbul tomorrow. Neutral.
