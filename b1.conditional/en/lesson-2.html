level −20 · if not

If not: -maz + sa

You know the negative aorist: gelmez — doesn't come. Just attach -se to it. Nothing else.

Root first. Then -maz or -mez, that's the “not.” Then -sa. Then the person. Four bricks, like in lesson one. One of them just has a “not” sign on it.

The big trap. “I don't come” is gelmem, the z disappears. But in a condition it comes back from vacation: gelmezsem. Always.

A real-life example: “if I don't come, don't wait for me.” Gelmezsem, beni bekleme. Sounds like a song. A sad one.

level −21 · sign

Eğer is a neon sign

Eğer means “if.” But the “if” already sits in -sa. Eğer is a sign over the door. You switch it on for volume, especially in a long sentence.

Eğer yağmur yağarsa, müzeye gideriz.

With eğer it's louder, and you hear a condition coming sooner.

Turn off the sign and the meaning stays. Turn off -sa and the meaning's gone. Eğer doesn't work without -sa.

In conversation Turks often skip eğer. In contracts and exams they switch it on. For gravitas. Not sure? Leave it out. It won't be a mistake.

level −22 · no verb

“If” without a verb

Tired, sick, home, a student. No verb, but you need an “if.” Then -sa attaches straight to the word. After a vowel, the -y- bridge steps in.

Word
Who

Same persons: -m, -n, nothing, -k, -nız, -lar. The -y- bridge is needed only when the word ends in a vowel: hasta → hastaysan.

It's an economy move. No need for “if you happen to be tired.” Just “if tired.” Even “at home” can do it: evdeysen.

The -y- bridge keeps two vowels from bumping heads.

level −23 · there is, there isn't

There is, there isn't, not

The three hardest-working words in any lease. Var — there is, yok — there isn't, değil — not. With -sa they become “if there is,” “if there isn't,” “if not.”

Değil ends in i, so -se: değilse. Var and yok have back vowels: -sa.

A lease has more of these than commas. Learn the three and half the lease is read.

level −24 · plan and dream

Plan or dream

Remember the landlady and the rain? Here's the whole difference. With the aorist, it's a plan: it can happen. Without it, it's a dream: “if only.”

PLAN · aorist + -sa
Yağmur yağarsa, müzeye gideriz.

If it rains, we'll go to the museum.

DREAM · keşke + -sa
Keşke yağmur yağsa!

If only it would rain!

The bar shows how real it is. Two letters move it more than the weather forecast.

You already know the dream from the last topic. Now the key is not mixing them up in front of the landlady. Scientists never measured the bar, by the way.

The landlady from lesson one will confirm: huge difference.

level −25 · capsules

Capsule phrases

Ready-made “ifs” Turks say a hundred times a day. Learn each one as a single word. Flip the card.

Istersen is a polite offer. Mümkünse is a polite request. The rest depend on the situation. Use them often. Turks will notice.

You can drop mümkünse into almost any request. It sounds softer and instantly more polite.

level −28 · lab

The “if not” lab

Four modes. “Yes / no” is a switch. “Word” is the -y- bridge. “Plan / dream” — pick one. “Lease” — build a clause.

Start with the switch. Tap it and listen to -maz appear. Then the bridge, then dreams, then the lease.

level −29 · traps

Five traps

Second level, second set of rakes to step on. Everyone steps on them. The point is to step on different ones.

01
Gelmemsem, beni bekleme.
Gelmezsem, beni bekleme.

In a condition the z comes back: -mez + se. No -mem.

02
Hastasan, evde kal.
Hastaysan, evde kal.

The word ends in a vowel, so you need the -y- bridge.

03
Keşke yağmur yağarsa!
Keşke yağmur yağsa!

Keşke is a dream. The aorist doesn't belong there.

04
Sorun var eğer, arayın.
Sorun varsa, arayın.

Eğer doesn't go in the tail instead of -sa. The tail is -sa.

05
Pahalı değilsa, alırım.
Pahalı değilse, alırım.

In değil the last vowel i is front. So -se.

The last two traps are about vowels. They sting. A one-letter mistake that sounds like an accent.
