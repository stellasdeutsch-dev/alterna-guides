level −0 · surface

Saturday in Antalya

Two letters. That's all that separates a plan from a dream.

You rented an apartment in Antalya. For a month. Sea view, AC, the landlady on WhatsApp.

The landlady texts. Asks what you're doing this weekend.

You want to answer: if it rains, we'll go to the museum. You remember -sa. You proudly type: Yağmur yağsa!

The landlady goes quiet for a long time. Then she writes that Antalya gets three hundred sunny days a year. A tourist who dreams of rain is news for the local paper.

You weren't dreaming. You were planning. The difference is two letters.

level −1 · scouting

Guess first

No rule yet. Just instinct. Nothing happens if you get it wrong. Not even rain.

If I come…

To come — gelmek. Aorist — gelir.

If we're late…

To be late — geç kalmak. Aorist — kalır.

Guessed right? Good. Guessed wrong? Also good. Your brain saved the question and is now waiting for the answer.

level −2 · two floors

Two floors

Every “if” is a two-story house. Upstairs, the condition. There's always -sa or -se in it. Downstairs, what will happen. Normal life down there: aorist, future, a request.

If it rains, we'll go to the museum.

The elevator runs between floors. The voice reads upstairs first, then downstairs. That's almost always the order.

English builds it the same way. Only our “if” hangs outside, like a sign. In Turkish it's inside the verb.

level −3 · assembly

Four-step assembly

Hit “Play.” Or flip through the steps yourself. Each step is one brick.

Take the root. To come — gel.

Add the aorist. You know it: gelir — comes, usually.

Attach -se. The last vowel i is front. So -se, not -sa.

Add the person. I is -m. Gelirsem — if I come.

Four bricks. The order never changes: root, tense, “if,” who. Mix it up and you get an art installation, not a house.

level −4 · foundation

Foundation: the aorist

The condition stands on the aorist. No foundation, no house. Here it is in a minute.

01

One syllable → -ar / -er

yap → yapar · git → gider

Almost all short verbs.

02

Stubborn ones → -ır / -ir / -ur / -ür

gel → gelir · al → alır · ol → olur · gör → görür

Thirteen exceptions. Know them by sight.

03

Long ones and vowel-final ones

konuş → konuşur · oku → okur

Long verbs take -ır by harmony. Vowel-final ones just take -r.

After the aorist the choice is easy. Last vowel a, ı, o, u — -sa. e, i, ö, ü — -se.

Can't remember a verb's aorist? Say “he usually…” out loud. Your mouth remembers better than your head.

level −5 · residents

Six residents

Each person has its own tail. Four follow the rules. Two don't.

We is -k: gelirsek. Not -iz. They: -ler goes before -se: gelirlerse. Two doors everyone walks into.

The other four are boring. They take their tail and live in peace.

level −6 · comparison

Five words against two

English: if I come, I call. Five words. Turkish: two. “If,” “I” and “come” moved into one verb.

5

level −7 · lower floor

What's on the lower floor

Upstairs is always -sa or -se. Downstairs is freedom. Four common options. Tap one and the elevator moves.

If you come tonight, we'll talk.

In this lesson upstairs gets only the aorist and -sa. Every other tense lives downstairs.

Want to ask? Ask. Want to promise? Promise. Want to plan? Plan. The lower floor doesn't care.

level −9 · lock

A lock in the door

The English “if” is a sign in front of the door. The Turkish one is a lock built into the door itself. At the end of the verb. Listen for it: -rsa, -rse.

You can take a sign down. Not a lock.

level −13 · traps

Five traps

Everyone trips on these. That's normal. Wishing rain on your landlady is not.

01
Eğer gelirim, ararım.
Eğer gelirsem, ararım.

Eğer is just a sign. -se makes the “if.” Without it, eğer means nothing.

02
Erken kalkarsız, yüzeriz.
Erken kalkarsak, yüzeriz.

We in a condition is -k. Not -iz.

03
Gelersem, ararım.
Gelirsem, ararım.

gel is a stubborn verb; its aorist is gelir. Crooked foundation, the house falls.

04
Yağmur yağarse, müzeye gideriz.
Yağmur yağarsa, müzeye gideriz.

The last vowel a is back. So -sa.

05
Yağmur yağsa, müzeye gideriz.
Yağmur yağarsa, müzeye gideriz.

Without the aorist it's a dream, not a plan. The landlady will think you want rain again.

The fifth one is the priciest. It already cost you your reputation.

The rain came. The landlady is sure you summoned it. Last time.

Word your wishes carefully. In Antalya they come true.
