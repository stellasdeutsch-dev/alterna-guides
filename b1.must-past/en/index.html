Step 02 · Form

Four bricks

Verb, “should,” “have,” person. Four tiles make the whole construction.

gelcomemelishouldydihavemI
almalıydım
gelcome melishould ydihave mI

Root: gel, to come.

Root first. Then -meli: “should.” Then -ydi: “have,” the past. Person at the end. The y is there because -meli ends in a vowel. Read it like English: gel-meh-leey-deem.

verb+mAlI+ydI+person

Harmony is lazy here: after -meli it’s always -ydi, after -malı always -ydı. Two options, not four. The root’s last vowel decides everything for you.

Step 03

Six persons, one pattern

Tap a cell to hear it. The switches change the verb and the kind of “have.”

“They” has two options: gelmeliydiler and gelmelilerdi. Both are correct; you’ll hear the first more often.

In English “should have” never changes: I, you, they, same words. In Turkish the person is the last suffix, and it changes every time. Ben is optional. The ending is not.

Step 04 · Meaning

Three shades of “should have”

Regret, reproach, and duty. One form, different intonation. Tap the points on the chart.

Pick a point

Each point is its own shade.
RegretAbout yourself: I should have, and I didn’t.
ReproachAbout someone else: you should have. Softer than yelling.
DutyJust a fact: I was supposed to be at work at eight.

The situation makes the shade, not the suffix. The same form can be care or a complaint. Your voice decides.

Step 05

Shouldn’t have

Put -me before -meli. You get “shouldn’t have.” In English “not” sticks to “should.” In Turkish it sticks to the verb: ye-me-meli.

Most often it’s regret after overdoing it: ate, spent, said.

Wherever the “not” sits, that’s the meaning. The switch below shows it in a second.

Bu kadar yememeliydim.I shouldn’t have eaten so much.
Bu kadar para harcamamalıydım.I shouldn’t have spent so much money.

Step 06

Or should I have?

Not sure? English flips the words: “should I have?” Turkish flips nothing. Put mi separately, and the person leaves with it.

doldurmalımıydım?
Bu formu doldurmalı mıydım?Should I have filled out this form?
Gelmemeli miydim?Maybe I shouldn’t have come?

People say the second one when they’re offended. A question on paper, a complaint in fact.

The usual answer to that kind of question: Evet, doldurmalıydın. Or calmly: Hayır, gerek yoktu. That is, “yes, you should have” or “no, there was no need.”

Step 07 · -ymış

Turns out, you should have

Swap -ydı for -ymış. Now you didn’t know. Someone told you, you read it, or you figured it out later.

Tap the news in the feed.

English “turns out” is an almost exact translation. If you feel like slipping it in, you need -ymış.

Same verb, same “should.” What changes is your experience: you didn’t see it. You learned it from the feed, the guide, or the pharmacist.

Step 08

Your own chart or someone else’s feed

Same words. The source changes: saw it yourself, or found out later.

In English “should have” doesn’t change. The source hides in words: “turns out,” “apparently.”
In Turkish the source sits inside the verb. One suffix changes: -ydı → -ymış. No extra word needed, and it follows harmony: çıkmalıymış, gelmeliymiş.

Step 11

Five traps

Five mistakes that come straight from English. Even people in their third year in Antalya make them. Tap and we’ll fix it. Good news: there are only five.

Trap 1

The missing person

Ben gelmeliydi.
Ben gelmeliydim.

“Should have” never changes in English, so the ending gets lost. In Turkish the person must be at the end: -m.

Trap 2

“Not” on the wrong word

Gelmeliydim değil.
Gelmemeliydim.

English: should + not. Turkish: verb + not, then -meli: gel-me-meli.

Trap 3

Broken harmony

almalıydim
almalıydım

English has no vowel harmony, Turkish does. After “malı,” only “ydı”, and the person is -ım.

Trap 4

The lost y

gelmelidim
gelmeliydim

Between two vowels you need a y buffer. Say it like the y in “yes”: meli-y-dim.

Trap 5

Question glued on

gelmelimiydim?
gelmeli miydim?

English flips words: “should I have?” Turkish doesn’t. It adds mi as a separate word, and the person goes with it.
