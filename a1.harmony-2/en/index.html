One tail. Two outfits.

Why the Turkish tail is sometimes -lar and sometimes -ler. And how to pick the right one in a second.

The Turkish tail never embarrasses itself like that. It carries two outfits. Before walking in, it checks whose party it is.

Rule

Capital A is an entertainer without a costume

Textbooks write -lAr and -DA. The capital A isn’t a typo. It’s a tail that hasn’t changed yet. It’ll put on a or e.

Three questions in order

The entertainer comes in from the end of the word. The first thing he sees is the last vowel. That’s what he dresses for.

  1. Find the last vowel.
  2. Is it a back or a front vowel?
  3. Back — the a outfit. Front — the e outfit.
back → a
front → e

The word’s other vowels don’t matter. Even if there are lots of them.

One rule

Four tails. One closet.

There are four tails with a capital A. What they mean comes in their own topics. For now, just watch the outfit.

-lAr · many
-DA · where
-DAn · from where
-(y)A · to where

Change the word — the outfit changes for all four at once. One closet for everyone.
