Program

Three modules, one verb

No new roots. You already know the infinitive. That's the whole magic.

MODULE 1

Take the whole infinitive.

gelmekto come
MODULE 2

Add -te. The verb is in progress.

gelmekteis coming (in a letter)
MODULE 3

For the news, hook on -dir.

gelmektediris coming (on the news)
RESULT

Take off the tie — you get something familiar.

gelmektedir=geliyoris coming
Proje hızla devam etmektedir.The project is moving forward fast.
Kurs 23 Ocak'ta başlamaktadır.The course starts January 23. No seats left.
İstanbul'da yağmur yağmaktadır.It's raining in Istanbul. Big surprise.

Harmony

The dog fetches the tail

Throw a bone with a verb. The dog will bring back the ending.

-mak → -maktadır. -mek → -mektedir. There are no other options.

The dog doesn't think. It looks at the last vowel and brings what's needed.

gelmektedir
a ı o u → -maktadıre i ö ü → -mektedir
Persons

Plans: who's talking

The news is almost always about “he” and “they.” In a letter you can talk about yourself too.

Tap a row — it'll speak. The person tail always comes at the very end.

English and Turkish

English wears a tie too

An official won't say “we're boarding.” They'll say “boarding is now in progress.” Turkish does the same thing, only shorter.

Memory trick

A verb in a tie

-te is the tie. -dir is the cap. Take both off — you get -iyor.

Gelmektedir sounds like a bureaucrat's last name. Remember it that way: Mr. Gelmektedir is arriving. Officially.
