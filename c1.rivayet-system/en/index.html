Scene 01 · Samsun, the firm

The witness who wasn’t there

A lesson in six scenes

-mış
-mişmiş

01Samsun, a landscape design firm. Artyom’s in his second year here. His Turkish is good, his coworkers say so.

01Morning. Selin whispers: Ali'ye göre toplantı çoktan bitmişmiş. According to Ali, the meeting ended long ago.

01Artyom goes to the boss. He says it with confidence: Toplantı çoktan bitmişti.

01The boss looks over his glasses: Sen orada mıydın? Were you there? Artyom wasn’t. Artyom was in the cafeteria.

Scene 02 · hallway news

Moss on any stone

Belediye yeni bir park yapıyormuş.They say the city is building a new park.

Park yazın açılacakmış.They say the park will open in the summer.

Selin her sabah sahilde koşarmış. Müdür bugün hastaymış.They say Selin runs every morning. The boss is sick today.

02Guess first. Four pieces of hallway news. What do they have in common?

02They all end in -mış. That’s moss. It says: I didn’t see it, someone told me.

02Moss grows on any tense: yapıyor → yapıyormuş, açılacak → açılacakmış, koşar → koşarmış.

02And on nouns: hasta → hastaymış. That’s how Turkish fairy tales begin: Bir varmış, bir yokmuş.

02Moss also means “turns out.” Müdür hastaymış — turns out the boss is sick. So that’s why the office is so quiet.

Pass on the news: Park yazın açılacak.

Scene 03 · the floating island

Double moss: -mişmiş

bitmişmiş

Ali'ye göre toplantı çoktan bitmişmiş.According to Ali, the meeting ended long ago.

Artyom: bitmişti “long ago, and I saw it myself”

03Now a stone that already has moss on it. bitmiş — ended long ago.

03A second layer grows on top: bitmişmiş. The first -miş is “long ago.” The second is “so Ali says.”

03Artyom said bitmişti. That’s “long ago, and I saw it myself.” The witness who wasn’t there.

03In conversation, double moss often sounds doubtful: Bitmişmiş! Ali toplantıya hiç gitmedi ki.

03If Ali had seen it all himself, he’d just say bitmişti. The second moss is added by whoever passes it on.

To the boss: “according to Ali, the meeting ended long ago”

Scene 04 · the tender office door

Turns out I should have

Meğer başvurular dün bitmiş.Turns out applications closed yesterday.

Daha erken başvurmalıymışım.Turns out I should have applied earlier.

Herkes kask takmalıymış.They say everyone has to wear a hard hat.

04Artyom goes to submit a bid for a tender. There’s a notice on the door. The deadline was yesterday.

04Meğer başvurular dün bitmiş. Meğer means “turns out.” Here the moss isn’t about rumors, it’s about a discovery.

04Daha erken başvurmalıymışım. Turns out I should have applied earlier. Only found out now.

04Compare: başvurmalıydım — I knew I had to. Just didn’t make it. A different kind of painful.

04And at the construction site they say: Herkes kask takmalıymış. Word is everyone needs a hard hat. Even the boss.

“Turns out I was supposed to come at eight”

Scene 05 · a letter overgrown with grass

“If only,” secondhand

Erken başvursaymışız ihaleyi kazanırmışız.(The boss says:) if we had applied earlier, we would have won the tender.

Erken başvursaydık ihaleyi kazanırdık.The same thing in your own words.

05In the evening Selin passes on what the boss said: Erken başvursaymışız ihaleyi kazanırmışız.

05If we had applied earlier, we would have won. But the boss said it, not Selin. So there’s moss on both halves.

05In your own words it would be: başvursaydık, kazanırdık. Burgundy -DI on both halves.

05The pair always travels together: -sAymIş in the condition, -(A/I)rmIş in the result. Moss grows on both stones at once.

gelmiş · geldiymiş

06Moss grows on every stone but one. It slides right off -DI: nobody says geldiymiş.

06Want to retell the past? Just use -mış: Ali dün gelmiş.

06That’s the whole system. Left — saw it myself. Right — secondhand. The forms come in matching pairs.

06Easy to remember: where there’s burgundy on the left, there’s moss on the right. The rest of the word stays the same.

Fix it: Ali dün geldiymiş.

Scene 08 · didn’t take root

Failed transplants

Everyone trips on these, even after ten years of Turkish. Four sprouts that didn’t take root.

Sprout 1

Ali dün geldiymiş.

Moss won’t stick to -DI. The past, secondhand, is just -mış.

Took rootAli dün gelmiş.

Sprout 2

Toplantı çoktan bitmişti. (about something you didn’t see)

-mıştı makes you a witness. The boss will ask where you were standing.

Erken başvursaydık kazanırmışız.

Half in your own words, half in someone else’s. Moss goes on both stones or on neither.

Sprout 4

Without -mış it’s advice for the future: “I need to apply.” But that train has left.

Scene 12 · the garden plan

Cheat sheet

Retelling

tense + -(y)mIş

any tense

geliyormuş · gelecekmiş · gelirmiş · hastaymış

they say: is coming, will come, comes, is sick

double moss

bitmişmiş

long ago and secondhand; in speech — with doubt

should have

başvurmalıymışım

turns out I should have; they say you have to

if only

başvursaymışız … kazanırmışız

“if…, then…” — secondhand

Left, saw it myself: -DI. Right, secondhand: -mIş. Moss doesn’t grow on -DI: not geldiymiş but gelmiş.

A week later the boss asks again: Toplantı bitti mi? Artyom answers: Ali'ye göre çoktan bitmişmiş. Ben orada değildim.

The boss nods. No questions. A month later Artyom got promoted. So they say. He didn’t see it himself.

Now you can retell in any tense: geliyormuş, bitmişmiş, başvurmalıymışım, başvursaymışız. And you don’t become a witness where you weren’t.
