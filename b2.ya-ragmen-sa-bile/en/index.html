Scene 2 · The window

Meet rağmen. A realist. Believes only what he can see out the window.

English says “despite the traffic”: despite first, the noun stays bare. Turkish flips it. Noun first, then the dative -(y)A (the “to” ending), then rağmen.

trafik → trafiğe. A k between vowels softens to ğ. You barely hear ğ: rağmen sounds like “raa-men.” After a vowel, y steps in: havaya.

Tired, but went to work anyway? Yorgunluğa rağmen. (A Monday classic.) Everything broke, and you’re still happy? Her şeye rağmen.

He has a brother, karşın. Same realist, just in a tie. The news and formal letters love him.

A whole action instead of a word? The verb puts on -mAsInA: kar yağmasına rağmen — despite it snowing. Like English, the verb turns into a noun.

Scene 3 · The forecast

And this is bile. A dreamer. The window bores him. He lives in the weather forecast.

His world is “what if.” So he needs the “if” form of the verb: gel → gelse. No separate word for “if”: it’s the ending.

The person goes at the back: gelsem, gelsen, gelse, gelsek. Then bile, a separate word. English puts “even if” first. Turkish puts bile last.

Kar yağsa bile yarın gelirim. No snow yet. But Arman is already heroically ready.

Instead of bile you can use dA: gelsem de, yağsa da. Also separate. The vowel follows the last syllable. Same meaning, bile is just a bit stronger.

The negation hides inside: gelmesem bile — even if I don’t come.

Scene 4 · Window or forecast

Guess first. Two sentences about the same rain. Which one is looking out the window?

Yağmura rağmen yürüdük. It rained, we walked. It happened. Window.

Yağmur yağsa bile yürürüz. No rain. But if it starts, we’ll walk anyway. Forecast.

The rule in one line: was or is — rağmen (“even though”). Might be — -sA bile (“even if”). English makes the same split.

Window and forecast

Window · was or is

noun + -(y)A + rağmen

  • kara · trafiğe · havaya rağmen
  • her şeye rağmen · buna rağmen
  • more formal: her şeye karşın
  • action: kar yağmasına rağmen
  • ≈ even though / despite

Forecast · might be

verb + -sA + person + bile / dA

  • gelsem bile · istesen de
  • kar yağsa da · pahalı olsa da
  • not: gelmesem bile
  • bile and dA are separate words
  • bile goes last, not first like “even if”

Was or is — rağmen. Might be — bile.

Now you can say “despite” about what already happened. And “even if” about what only might happen. Kızılay traffic won’t throw you off anymore.
