Stop 2Home, trip, business trip
Stop 2Three meanings of one word
The word’s route

kök travels to three places

gerçekağacın köküthe root of a tree
mecazsorunun köküthe root of the problem
terimkareköksquare root

A word has a home, and it takes trips.

Home is gerçek anlam, the literal meaning: ağacın kökü, the root of a tree. You can touch it.

A trip is mecaz anlam, the figurative meaning: sorunun kökü, the root of the problem. You can’t touch it, but you get it.

A business trip into science is terim anlam: karekök, square root. The meaning belongs to the subject.

The test is simple: can you weigh it, pour it, see it? Then it’s literal. Ağır çanta, yes. Ağır söz, ağır hastalık, no. That’s figurative.

Stop 3A familiar landscape
Stop 3Resemblance things as people
dağın eteğifoot of the mountain dağın başısummit

kapı koludoor handle şişenin ağzıbottle neck
masa + ayak + ı = masa ayağı

The first road to figurative meaning is resemblance. A Turk looks at a thing and sees a person in it.

A mountain wears a skirt: dağın eteği. The lower slopes spread out like a hem.

A table has legs: masa ayağı. A door has an arm: kapı kolu. A bottle has a mouth: şişenin ağzı.

It’s built on the familiar two-noun pattern: masa + ayak + ı. Not someone’s leg, just a “table leg” in general.

English does the same, just picks different body parts: the leg of a table, the foot of a mountain, the neck of a bottle.

What does this thing have? bottle

Stop 4Place for people
Stop 4Proximity borrowing the neighbor’s name
CONCERT · SAMSUNBütün salon ayağa kalktı.

Ankara= the government

The second road is proximity. A word takes the name of whatever stands next to it.

A concert in Samsun:Bütün salon ayağa kalktı.The hall didn’t stand up. The people in it did.

The news:Ankara bu konuda henüz karar vermedi.It’s not the city deciding, it’s the government sitting in it.

This is ad aktarması, a name transfer. English does it too: “the White House said,” “the room applauded.”

Bütün Samsun sokaklara döküldü: it wasn’t the asphalt pouring into the streets, it was the townspeople.

Who is it really? Ankara karar verdi.

Proximity has lots of addresses. The four main ones are in Kemal Bey’s kitchen.

Container for contents:Çaydanlık kaynıyor.The water is boiling. In English the kettle boils too.

İki bardak içtim.I drank the tea, the glass is fine.

Author for books:Yaşar Kemal'i okudum.Read the novels, not the biography. Like “I’ve read Dickens.”

Tool for skill:Kemal Bey'in kalemi kuvvetli.He writes brilliantly. In English: he has a sharp pen.

Part for person:Evde dört boğaz var.Four mouths to feed. English uses the mouth, Turkish the throat. And another one: İki el daha lazım, we need two more hands.

What is it really? Çaydanlık kaynıyor.

Can you say “as if”?
masa ayağıAnkara karar verdidağın eteğiiki bardak içtim

How do you keep the roads apart? One question.

Can you slip in “as if”? A table’s leg is as if it were a person’s leg. So it’s resemblance, mecaz.

Does the link answer “where, in what, whose, with what”? People in the hall, tea in the glass, Yaşar Kemal’s books. So it’s proximity, ad aktarması.

Resemblance is a photo. Proximity is an address.

Looks like, or next to? masa ayağı

Yes means resemblance. An answer to “where, in what, whose, with what” means proximity.

A month later. Same negotiations. Kemal Bey brings up the salary again.

Aigerim: “He has four mouths to feed at home.” The director nods. The salary goes up.

Kemal BeyAğzına sağlık!Literally “health to your mouth,” meaning “well said.” The mouth instead of the words: proximity again.

Now you see a word’s home, its trips and its business trips. And you translate the meaning, not the throat.
