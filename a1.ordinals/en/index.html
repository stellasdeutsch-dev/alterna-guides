Number + tail

Take the number. Look at the last vowel. Sew on -ncı in the right outfit. Say c like the j in jam: birinci sounds like “bee-rin-jee.” And ı is the a in about.

You tell the cashier: üçüncü sıra. She says: tamam. Third row. Movies. No adventures.

Four outfits

The tail dresses according to the number’s last vowel. Four-way harmony — you already know it. English -th wears the same thing every day. The Turkish tail is a fashionista.

English ↔ Turkish

English bends words. Turkish doesn’t

English has one tail, -th, but it keeps breaking the word: one → first, two → second, five → fifth. Turkish never touches the number. It just adds -ncı. The price: four outfits instead of one. The tail checks the last vowel, like every Turkish suffix.

English: one tail, many odd words. Turkish: four outfits, one odd word — dördüncü.

After a vowel — nothing extra

Turkish doesn’t like two vowels in a row. If the number ends in a vowel, the tail’s first letter disappears. Saving on letters is still saving.

Dört softens

dört

Before the tail’s vowel, t gets tired and turns into d. Only in four. English does this too, just elsewhere: five → fifth, twelve → twelfth.

You say dörtüncü kat. The elevator operator winces. You say dördüncü kat. The elevator operator smiles. The elevator moves.

  • Dördüncü kat.The fourth floor.
  • Yirmi dördüncü gün.The twenty-fourth day.

Big numbers: the tail goes to the last one

Like in English: “twenty-first.” Only the last word changes. But no hyphen: yirmi birinci. Drag the slider. A hundred floors — no elevator, but with grammar.

Dot = tail

English writes 3rd. Turkish just puts a dot: 3. kat, read üçüncü kat. Without the dot it’s just three. One dot, two floors of difference.

Kaç? or Kaçıncı?

Kaç is “how many.” Kaçıncı is “how-many-th” — a word English doesn’t have. You say “which floor?” Turkish asks for the number in line. Tap a question — the building will answer.

You say kaç kat? You get a number. You say kaçıncı kat? You get an address. The difference — three letters and half an hour of your life.

Number + -(I)ncI: ıncı, inci, uncu, üncü.
After a vowel — -ncI: ikinci, altıncı.
