-(y)AnA kadar and -(y)IncAyA kadar: until

Two forms, one boundary, and not a single “not.” Today we learn to set a boundary: an action keeps going until something happens. Scroll down — the ball's already in play.

story

A court in Çankaya

Ankara. A padel club in Çankaya. Saturday, eight a.m. At this hour even the pigeons are asleep. You're not.

Your partner is late. Five minutes, ten, fifteen. You type in Turkish, but your head is still translating.

You write: Sen gelmeyene kadar bekliyorum.

He reads it. For a while. Writes back: Peki. Gelmiyorum.

Totally fair. You're waiting until he doesn't come. He doesn't come. Wait complete.

You're standing on the court alone. With a ball. Exactly as you requested.

blueprint

Building the form piece by piece

rootgel

-Ae
kadarkadar
dur → duranaoku → okuyanagit → gidenede → diyene

Take the verb root, meaning the boundary event. For example, gel- — to come.

Add -en. You get “the moment of coming,” a point on the line.

Then the dative -e: “to that moment.” You already know it from akşama. Here the dative means heading toward the boundary.

And kadar — “all the way to.” Done: gelene kadar.

Two-way harmony: a or e. After a vowel, add y. A voiceless t turns voiced: gidene.

You already know gelince — “as soon as they come.”

Tack on -ye kadar and you get “all the way to the moment they come.”

Same meaning as gelene kadar. Sounds a bit more bookish, like a public notice. School rules especially love it.

trap number one

Where's the “not”?

I'll wait until you DON'T come
Sen gelene kadar bekliyorum.

Some languages slip a “not” in here on autopilot: wait until you don't come. English doesn't — good instinct.

Turkish hears that “not” — and smashes it off the court.

What's left is Sen gelene kadar bekliyorum. No -me- at all.

The boundary is always positive: whatever has to happen, that's what you write. No negatives, no fear.

two lines

Tense lives in the main verb

main actionbekliyorumwaiting right now
boundarysen gelene kadar

The boundary doesn't change: sen gelene kadar. No person, no tense. It's like the net — it stays put.

The main verb changes: I'm waiting now, I usually wait, I'll wait. Want “I waited”? Change only that.

And the boundary's subject is plain: sen, Ali, ders. No genitive -in.

noun or verb

Akşama kadar or akşam olana kadar

You can already do it with a noun: akşama kadar — until evening.

Need a verb — use -(y)AnA kadar: akşam olana kadar — until evening comes. Almost the same meaning, but now the boundary is an event.
