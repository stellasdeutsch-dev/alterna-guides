Rule 1 · distance

Once upon a time there were three brothers

All three point at things. Just one difference. Distance.

bu

The homebody. Everything in his hands. This.

range

şu

He points. That one there, next to you.

range

o

The dreamer. Looks into the distance. Whatever is far away or not here at all.

range

Tap a brother. He’ll say his word.

The brothers go right before the word. Like English “this” and “that.”

  • bu bu çay this tea, with me
  • şu şu çay that tea there, with you
  • o o çay that tea, far away

Rule 2 · the question

Ne or kim?

ne is “what.” kim is “who.”

Guess first. A cat is sitting on the pier. How do you ask about it?

Tap a word. It’ll fly into its column by itself.

kim

people only

ne

everything else

Teacher, captain, Ali: kim. Book, bagel and cat: ne.

The answer takes the question’s spot

Bu ne?
↓
Bu bir kitap.

The word order doesn’t change. No “is.” bir works like English “a.” You can skip it.

O kim? Who is he? O öğretmen. He’s a teacher.

Eminönü. A simit cart

sellerBu bir simit.It’s a simit. A sesame bagel.
youŞu ne?And that one, by you?
sellerŞu bir poğaça.That one’s a pastry.
youO ne?And that, way over there?
sellerO bir vapur.That’s a ferry. It’s yours.
youO kim?And who’s he?
sellerO kaptan.He’s the captain.
Tips from a survivor

Three traps

A cat is not kim. Even if he runs the place.

Bu kim? about a cat. Hurts the ear.

The right way: Bu ne? ✓

O has no gender. O kim? It’s both “who is he?” and “who is she?”.

Which one, the situation will tell you.

Şu doesn’t mean “far.” Şu is what you point your finger at. Next to you or to the person you’re talking to.

Far away or out of sight: o.

Listen to native speakers

Notice where the thing is when you hear bu, şu or o.
