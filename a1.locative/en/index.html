01 · Foundation

Three prepositions. One box.

In English a place needs a little word in front: in the house, on the roof, at the door. Three words, and you have to pick the right one. Turkish can’t be bothered. It takes one tail, -DA, and sticks it on the end of the word.

    Last vowel a ı o u → -da. Last vowel e i ö ü → -de. The tail adapts to the walls.

    03 · Roof

    Voiceless consonants mute the d

    Does the word end in one of eight letters? Then d turns into t. The eight letters hide in a phrase:

    Fıstıkçı Şahap

    “Şahap the pistachio seller.” Sounds like the foreman’s name.

    The vowel in the tail doesn’t change; harmony works the same as before: mutfakta, işte.

    04 · Sign

    Names get a nail

    Cities, neighborhoods, and people’s names start with a capital, same as in English. In Turkish they also get an apostrophe before the tail. Like a nail between the sign and the wall. English: in Istanbul. Turkish: İstanbul'da.

    Harmony and Fıstıkçı Şahap work here too: Beşiktaş'ta.

    05 · Tenants

    Who lives where

    English needs a separate am / is / are: I’m at home. Turkish has no such word. It glues the person onto the end: ev-de-yim, “house-at-I-am.” After a vowel, add the buffer y. o is he, she and it at once, and it gets no ending at all.
