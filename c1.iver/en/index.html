Hook

The race for the check

You're in Atakum. Pide's gone, the sea's watching, the sun's setting. Time to pay.

You ask the waiter nicely, textbook-style. He nods and vanishes. Forever.

Twenty minutes go by. You manage to grow old and learn the menu by heart.

At the next table, an old man. Raises a finger, says one sentence. The check flies in twenty seconds later.

ver
The rule

Stem + ı + ver

Take a verb. Add a link. Then ver — “to give”. From there ver lives like a regular verb: tense, person, question.

açstem ılink verturbo ditense

aç — the stem: “to open”.

Ali kapıyı bir anda açıverdi.Ali suddenly went and opened the door.

Literally: “give opening”. Only there's no one to give to. Here ver works as an accelerator.

Written as one word. Always. “Açı ver” is a mistake.

The link

A run-up by harmony

The link is a high vowel following harmony: açıver, geliver, oturuver, görüver.

Pick a verb

After a vowel, y steps in: okuyuver, bekleyiver. The consonant t turns voiced: gidiver, hallediver.

Sounds long? Try saying it without the link. Your tongue will trip on its own. The link is grease.

Tense

Ver — in every tense

Ver acts like the most ordinary verb. Need a request, a story or a promise — only the tail changes.

There's a negative too: gitmeyiver — “oh, just don't go”. That's not speed anymore, it's “forget it”.

just
Meaning 1

“Just”: an offhand request

A request with -iver sounds like it's no big deal. A second for you, a joy for me.

This is for your people: friends, family, a neighbor. With a boss — no turbo.

Açıversene is already impatience. That's how you ask the second time, looking them in the eye.

Tuzu veriverir misin?Could you just pass the salt?
Şu çantayı tutuver.Just hold this bag.
Bir dakika bekleyiver.Just wait a minute.
Açıversene!Come on, open it! (impatiently)
Meaning 2

“Suddenly went and…”

In the past, -iverdi is sudden and unexpected. Nobody saw it coming. Bam — done.

Bam — done.

English says “up and left”. Turkish says çıkıverdi. One word instead of three.

Next to it you'll often see birden, bir anda, hemen. It's like a siren: -iverdi is coming.

Ali bir anda çıkıverdi.Ali suddenly up and left.
Tabak yere düşüverdi.The plate suddenly fell on the floor.
Çocuk ağlayıverdi.The child suddenly burst into tears.

Meaning 3

“In a sec”

With the aorist, -iver promises: I'll do it easily, no problem. Sounds modest and confident at once.

The neighbor who fixes a faucet in a minute says exactly this. And fixes it.

Merak etme, ben hallediveririm.Don't worry, I'll sort it out in a sec.
Seni istasyona bırakıveririm.I'll drop you off at the station.
Akşam size uğrayıveririm.I'll swing by your place tonight.

Comparison

English, Turkish, Kazakh

In English, little add-ons carry these shades: “just”, “went and”, “in a sec”. In Turkish — one verb on top.
