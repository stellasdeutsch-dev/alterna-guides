The country is the ball. The suffix -lı is the shot. After the shot, the ball flies into the “where from” hole.

Kazakistan + lı = Kazakistanlı. From Kazakhstan. A Kazakhstani.

In English every city has its own quirk: New Yorker, Londoner, Parisian, Muscovite. Turkish doesn't do quirks. One club for every city.

In Kazakh, by the way, it's similar: almatylyq.

Now the wind. The -lı club comes in four kinds.

The word's last vowel decides. Only that one, the rest stay quiet. On the board, each pair of vowels has its own club.

İstanbul ends in u. So it's İstanbullu. Say İstanbullı — and the ball's in the bushes.

It's the same vowel harmony. The shot adjusts to the wind.

Remember “I'm a teacher”? Öğretmenim. The “I, you, we” tail sticks to the end of the word.

Same here. Nere + li + sin. Literally “where-from-ish you?” Sounds funny, works great. For “he, she” there's no tail: O İzmirli.

-lı always ends in a vowel. So a y goes before -ım: Kazakistanlıyım.

To a stranger — politely: Nerelisiniz?

HOLE 5Who you are
Amerikalı · Çinli

Evening. A hostel in Beyoğlu. Everyone's getting to know each other.

Many peoples have their own word: Rus, Alman, Japon, Türk. No suffixes at all.

It's like “Russian” by ethnicity vs. “from Russia.” Rus is the nationality. Rusyalı means from Russia, by passport or address.

No word of its own — take the country and -lı: Amerikalı, Çinli.

Morning. Kadıköy. Damir asks for a menu. The waiter checks:İngilizce? Rusça?

DamirRusça, lütfen. Kazakça ve Rusça biliyorum.I speak Kazakh and Russian. biliyorum means “I know” — take it whole.

Language = people + -ca. Not from the country, from the people: Rus → Rusça.

Vowel: a, ı, o, u → -ca. e, i, ö, ü → -ce.

After voiceless consonants, c devoices to ç. The voiceless ones hide in the phrase Fıstıkçı Şahap.

Kazakh does the same: qazaqşa, orysşa.
