The bus at five

    (01)

    Antalya Museum · statue hall

    One ending separates “must” from “maybe.” Dasha finds out in the parking lot.

    15:00

    Dasha has arrived in Antalya. First stop — the museum. Statues from ancient Perge.

    Selim the guide gathers the group at the entrance. Says one sentence and goes off for tea.

    SelimSaat beşte otobüste olmalısınız!You must be on the bus at five!

    Dasha hears “-malı” and decides it means “maybe.” Maybe at five. Maybe at six.

    The statues are beautiful. Dasha looks at every one. At five fifteen she walks out to the parking lot.

    No bus. Selim wasn’t joking.

    The -malı bracelet

    (02)

    How it works

    A museum ticket goes on your wrist. This bracelet goes on a verb.

    Guess first: okumalısın

    okustem: okumak minus -mak
    malıthe “must” bracelet
    sınwho: you
    a ı o u → malıoku · yat · ol
    e i ö ü → meligel · git · bekle

    Evening. Dasha is at the hotel; the taxi back ate half her budget. She opens the textbook.

    Guess first. Tap right in the frame.

    The word splits into strips. The stem, then the -malı bracelet, then the person ending.

    The stem is the verb without -mak: okumak → oku, gelmek → gel.

    There are two bracelets, picked by the stem’s last vowel. Back vowels take malı, front vowels take meli.

    Put the bracelet on: yatmak → yat + ?

    Tickets for everyone

    (03)

    Who has to

    After the bracelet comes the person ending. You already know it from “I am a student.”

    olmalıyımI must
    olmalıyızwe must
    olmalısınyou must
    olmalısınızyou (pl.) must
    olmalıhe, she must
    olmalılarthey must
    olbe
    malımust
    sınızyou (pl.)

    Next — who exactly has to. The endings from “I am a student” do the work: öğrenciyim.

    The bracelet ends in a vowel. So for “I” and “we” a y steps in at the seam: olmalıyım, olmalıyız.

    “He, she” gets no ending at all. “They” gets -lar.

    Dasha takes Selim’s sentence apart. ol-malı-sınız: you must be. No “maybe” anywhere.

    Dasha writes in her diary: “I need to go to bed early today.” Bu akşam erken ___.

    Signs and questions

    (04)

    Must not and do I have to

    The museum talks to visitors through signs. Almost all of them use -malı.

    dokuntouch
    manot
    malımust
    sınızyou (pl.)
    Heykellere dokunmamalısınız.Do not touch the statues.
    Flaş kullanmamalısınız.No flash photography.
    Buradahere
    bekle·meliwait · must
    miyim?question + I

    In the morning Dasha goes back to the museum. Now she reads the signs carefully.

    Negation is ma or me right before the bracelet: dokun-ma-malı-sınız.

    That’s how every museum ban sounds. Polite, but no options.

    A question is the separate word mi, and the person ending moves onto it. Burada beklemeli miyim?Do I have to wait here?

    A guard tells Dasha, “You can’t eat here.” Burada ___.

    Should or must be

    (05)

    Two meanings

    Just like English “must,” the bracelet can give orders and it can also guess.

    01

    Should, have to

    Daha çok kitap okumalısın.

    You should read more.

    02

    Must be, probably

    Selim otobüste olmalı.

    Selim must be on the bus.

    In the museum Dasha looks for Selim. He’s not there. Turns out the bracelet has two meanings.

    The first — should, have to. Advice or duty: you should read more.

    The second — a guess. I don’t know for sure, but I think so.

    A guess is almost always built with olmak: evde olmalı, yorgun olmalı. Like English “must be.”

    Ahmet çok yorgun olmalı. This is…

    Five sharp

    (11)

    Finale and cheat sheet

    Form

    • stem + malı / meli + person
    • okumalıyım · gelmelisin · olmalı

    Persons

    • -yım, -sın, —, -yız, -sınız, -lar
    • olmalıyız · olmalılar

    Must not

    • stem + ma/me + malı
    • dokunmamalısınız · gelmemeliyim

    Question and meaning

    • beklemeli miyim?
    • should: okumalısın · must be: evde olmalı

    Now you say “should,” “must not,” and “must be” with one bracelet.

    A week later — a trip to Perge. At sixteen to five Dasha gathers the group herself.

    DashaSaat beşte otobüste olmalıyız!We have to be on the bus at five!

    SelimAferin! Sen rehber olmalısın!Well done! You should be a guide!
