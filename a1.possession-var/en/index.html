What should you have said?

“I have” isn't “I am.” A Turk puts it differently: my reservation exists.

Guess first

What does it say?

Arabam var. The word araba means car. What does the whole sentence mean?

Hint: the tail -m at the end. That's the owner's tag on the thing.

Didn't get it — fine. Your brain is already hooked. The answer is one screen down.

Module 1

My thing + var

Turkish doesn't say “I have.” It says: my thing exists. There's no verb “to have” at all.

In English that sounds stiff: “my car exists.” For Turks, it's just how you talk.

Step 1. Who's the owner? Benim — my.

(benim)+thing+tag+var

You can drop Benim. The tag -m already says whose car it is: Arabam var.

Benim stays for emphasis. I have one. You — no idea.

Pick your ownership plan

Six plans. Each has its own owner and its own tag. Change the thing up top.

Tap a plan — hear the sentence. The plan's price is its tag. It doesn't get cheaper.

You already know the tags: they're possessive suffixes. After a vowel they're shorter: arabam, but evim.

Four languages

Russian borrows “at me”

Kazakh works exactly the same way: meniñ köligim bar. If you know Kazakh, this topic is already yours.

English reaches for the verb have. Turkish doesn't take a verb. The word var is enough.

Module 2

No? Cross out var

Negation with no new rules. The tag stays. Only the last word changes: var → yok. Tap the wall.

Turkish has more than one “no.” When something isn't there — only yok.

The phrase Sorun yok means “no problem.” In Istanbul you hear it a hundred times a day.

Module 3

Question var mı?

The question is built with the particle mı. You know it. The tag belongs to whoever you're asking.

EElif · Kadıköy

You can answer with a full sentence. But a Turk will usually say one word: Var. or Yok.

After a number — singular: İki kardeşim var. No -ler.

No owner — no tag: Wi-Fi var mı? That's just “is there…”

Algorithm

Three steps in order

Owner first. Then the thing with its tag. At the end — there, not there, or a question. Pick a sentence.

The order never changes. Only the words inside do.

Not I exist. The thing exists

Remember: in this sentence the thing exists, not you. You just sign it. Tap the spray can.

Say “ben var” and you confirm you exist. That's already obvious.
