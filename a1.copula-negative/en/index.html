Değil and questions in noun sentences

You already know “I’m a teacher.” Today, two new tricks: “I’m not a teacher” and “are you a teacher?”

The scissors cut off the “yes.” The key opens the question. The tail moves every time. Try it right here.

01
story

A pharmacy in Kadıköy

A pharmacy in Kadıköy. You stopped in for a Band-Aid. Just a Band-Aid.

The pharmacist looks at you over his glasses. He says, Hasta mısınız?

You know hayır. You know hasta. You glue them together.

You say, Hayır, hastayım.

He nods. Silently. Puts three boxes of pills on the counter. And a syrup.

You meant “no, I’m not sick.” You said “no, I’m sick.”

The Band-Aid stayed on the shelf.

03
rule one

Scissors: değil

Watch the hands. The phrase Yorgunum means “I’m tired.”

Cut off the tail. Put değil after the word. The tail jumps onto değil. Done.

English “not” leans on a helper: am not, is not. Turkish değil trails behind the word. And drags the tail along like a suitcase.

  1. Step one: we have the phrase “I’m tired.”
  2. Step two: the scissors cut off the tail.
  3. Step three: değil goes after the word.
  4. Step four: the tail jumps onto değil.

04
six persons

Değil by person

Değil has one vowel: i. Nobody to argue with. So the tail always has i.

Change the word. The word changes. Değil and its tail don’t budge.

Russian ↔ Turkish

In English “not” sits between “am” and the word. In Turkish değil comes after the word, and the tail rides on it.

If you know Kazakh, it’s on your side here: emes works the same way. But in questions they part ways.

07
rule two

Key: question

You know the key mı. Four forms: mı, mi, mu, mü, picked by the word’s last vowel.

Only one thing is new. The tail moves onto the key. Öğretmensin → Öğretmen misin?

Keep this order in your head: word, key, tail. When two vowels meet, a y goes between them: Hazır mıyım?

08
rule three

Scissors + key

A question with “not”: scissors first, then the key. The tail sits on the key.

After değil the key is always mi. The last vowel there is i. No options.

And değil mi? at the end of a phrase is like “right?” or “isn’t it?”

Quick question: “aren’t you hungry?” is…

09
answer

Hayır isn’t değil

Hayır is the “no” at the start of an answer. Değil is the “not” inside the phrase. “Yes” is evet plus the tail on the word.

A lone hayır without değil sounds like the pharmacy: “no, I’m sick.” You need both.

The tail rides in the last car

The word, değil, mi are cars of an Istanbul tram. The tail is a passenger. It always rides in the last one.

Sit in the wrong car and a Turk will still understand you. But he’ll smile.
