how to become a sweater

A Kazakh student gets into a taxi. The driver looks in the mirror.

He asks: Rus musun? The student wants to say “I’m Kazakh.” He answers: Kazağım.

The driver goes quiet. For about ten seconds.

Taxifirst month in Istanbul
2 / 3

Then he tugs at his own collar: bu kazak.

Kazak is Turkish for sweater. Kazağım is “I’m Kazakh.” Or “my sweater.” Depends on your luck.

The driver heard: I’m a sweater.

He laughed all the way home. Charged the usual fare.

But that’s not the point. Turkish has no “I am.” It has the tail -ım.

And it sticks to anything. Even a sweater.

Step 1 · how it works

In Turkish “to be” isn’t a word. It’s a tail

English needs “am”: I am a student. Turks throw it out. You can throw out “I” too. The tail says it all.

The third person has no tail. O öğrenci. He’s a student. That’s it. The laziest form in the language.

English can’t skip the “is” in “he is a student.” Turkish skips it without a second thought.

Step 3 · harmony

The tail is a chameleon. It looks at the last vowel and changes color

Nothing to memorize here: four pairs. Pick a person, tap a card, and you’ll hear it.

hastayım
a ı → ı
I’m sick

“They” has a simpler tail: -lar or -ler, just like the plural.

The “you” tails start with s. So they don’t need the buffer y: hastasın, hastasınız.
