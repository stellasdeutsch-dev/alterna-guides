03

course syllabus

Two routes from point A

In direct speech, modality sounds different. “You need to” is gerek. “Do it!” is a request.

In reported speech, each has its own route. Need goes to gerektiğini. A request goes to the accusative and the verb istedi. Tap a route.

04

module 1 · need

How “you need to” gets reported

-mA turns a verb into a noun. “To rest” becomes “resting.”

-m, -n, -sI is the person. It tells you whose need it is.

gerektiğini is the packaging for reported speech. The same gerek plus the familiar tail -DIğInI.

The tail gerektiğini never changes. Not for the person, not for the verb. It's concrete.

  1. Point A: the doctor said «Dinlenmen gerek».
  2. The verb becomes a noun: dinlen-me.
  3. The person is whose need it is. I'm reporting: dinlenme-m.
  4. We package gerek: gerektiğini.
  5. The reporting verb. Point B is ready.

What in dinlenmem gerektiğini says “me”?

05

the big twist

The reporter picks the person

The doctor says “you.” But you're the one reporting. So the need is yours, which means “mine”: -m.

You don't count the person from the direct speech. You count it from where the reporter stands. Pick who the doctor said it to.

The teacher told you all: «Çalışmanız gerek». You're telling Mom. How?

07

module 2 · asked

Mom asked

A direct request is “do it!” or “I want you to.” In reported speech it's the same -mA with a person.

Only instead of gerektiğini you get the accusative -I and a verb. The verb sets the tone.

The most common is istedi. The most polite is rica etti. The strictest is söyledi.

08

comparison

Need or asked?

The meanings are close. The grammar is different.

gerektiğini söyledi is about necessity. That's how a doctor, a teacher, the law talks.

-mI istedi is about someone's wish. That's how a mom, a friend, a neighbor talks.

The test is simple. Can you say “need to”? Then it's route one. Can you say “asked”? Route two.

09

module 3 · negatives

Two minuses, two meanings

You can put the minus in two places. And the meaning flips completely.

Minus on the verb: gitmemem gerektiğini — “I must not go.” The lock is shut.

Minus on gerek: gitmem gerekmediğini — “I don't need to go.” The lock is open: go if you want.

A request with a minus: gitmememi istedi — “asked me not to go.”

The doctor: no need for surgery. How do you report it?

10

three “musts”

gerek, lazım, şart

“Need to” has three faces. They all report about the same way.

gerek turns into gerektiğini by itself. lazım and şart aren't verbs, so they need a helper: olduğunu.

şart is the strictest. No options, like an exam on Monday.

The strictness doesn't get lost in reporting. It moves along with the word.

English ↔ Turkish

Where did “that” go?

English reports with a whole clause: said that I need to rest.

Turkish folds it into one noun with tails. “That” and “so that” disappear. -DIğInI and the accusative do their job.
