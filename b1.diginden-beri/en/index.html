Meaningrule 1

An event — and on until today

Something happened at one point. A move, a first meeting, a first job. And then something has stretched all the way to today.

The red dot is the event. The bar is what continues. The dashed line on the right is now.

The dot is set by a verb with the -dığımdan tail. The word beri stretches the bar to today.

If the start is just a date, the familiar -DAn beri is enough. If the start is an event with a verb, you need the whole construction.

Formrule 2

Five pieces of one word

Verb, -DIk, whose, -DAn and beri. Like a Lego set, except every piece means something.

The pieces always go in the same order. Swap them and the car won’t drive. The trickiest spot is step two. Before a vowel, k softens into ğ: geldik turns into geldiğ-.

Harmonyrule 3

Eight variants of one tail

-DIk adapts to the verb twice.

The vowel follows harmony: ı, i, u or ü. The consonant is d or t.

After the voiceless f, s, t, k, ç, ş, h, p you write t: içtiğimden, gittiğimden. Everywhere else, d.

Eight cells look scary. In reality you only look at two letters of the verb: the last vowel and the last consonant.

Tap a verb and its cell lights up.

Personsrule 4

Six people, six tails

The person tail sits in the middle of the word. These are the possessive suffixes, and you already know them.

Watch out for two of them. “You” and “he” sound the same: geldiğinden. Only context tells them apart. Or a pronoun at the start.

With “they,” k doesn’t soften: geldiklerinden. After k comes the consonant l, not a vowel.

Second halfrule 5

What comes after beri

After beri comes the main news. Whatever has lasted till today.

Most often it’s the -yor present: the action is going on right now.

Option two is “not once since”: hiç plus a negative past.

Option three is a state. Happy, calm, tired.

Traps

Six traps. Everyone falls in.

English whispers in your ear and puts words in the wrong place. Tap “Fix” and the red pen sorts it out.

01 · Bare verb
Antalya'ya geldim beri…
Antalya'ya geldiğimden beri…Ever since I came to Antalya…

The past tense doesn’t hook onto beri. You need the whole tail.

02 · Lost beri
Geldiğimden çok mutluyum.
Geldiğimden beri çok mutluyum.Ever since I came, I’ve been very happy.

Without beri, -dığımdan means “because.” You get “happy because I came.”

03 · Negation
Onu görmediğimden beri…
Onu iki yıldır görmüyorum.I haven’t seen him for two years.

Turks don’t build “ever since I didn’t…” They take a duration, -DIr and negation.

04 · Hard k
geldikimden beri
geldiğimden beriever since I came

Before a vowel, k falls into the water and becomes ğ.

05 · English word order
Beri taşındığımdan denize giriyorum.
Taşındığımdan beri denize giriyorum.Ever since I moved, I swim in the sea.

Beri goes after the verb, not before it. Turkish reads “backwards.”

06 · “They”
Evlendiğilerinden beri…
Evlendiklerinden beri Lara'da oturuyorlar.Ever since they got married, they live in Lara.

With “they,” k is followed by l. Nothing to soften: evlendikleri.
