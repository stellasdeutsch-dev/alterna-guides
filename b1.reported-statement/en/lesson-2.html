Elif

Elif tells Murat: Seni seviyorum. I love you.

Murat → you

Murat runs over to you. Says: Elif beni sevdiğini söyledi. Elif said she loves me.

You → Ayşe

You tell Ayşe: Elif'in Murat'ı sevdiğini duydum. I heard Elif loves Murat.

Antalya

By evening the whole neighborhood knows. Elif, by the way, said it to the cat. The cat's name is Murat.

One phrase. Three reports. And something changed in each: seni became beni, Elif'in and duydum showed up.

Reporting doesn't just change the verb. It changes pronouns, cases and even the speech verb.

söyledi / duydum / biliyorum / Elif'in geldiğini / beni sevdiğini / gelmemi söyledi / geleceğini söyledi /

Not just söyledi

The same form works with other verbs too. Tap a word.

Only the last verb changes. It says how you know the news: someone told you, it reached you, or you already knew.

Who did it

If one person speaks and another did it, the other one gets -nin.

Like “Ali's book”: Ali'nin kitabı. Only instead of a book, an action: Ali'nin geldiği.

Is the speaker the same person as the doer? Then no -nin. Different people — you need it. Switch and watch the arrow.

Story 03Reflective surfacesp. 04

Mirror

Reporting is a mirror. “You” in someone else's mouth becomes “me.”

Elif told Murat “you.” Murat reports it — and says “me.” Otherwise it sounds like Elif loves you.

Test yourself with one question: who's talking right now? Pick “me” or “you” from their point of view.

Story 04Two optical choicesp. 05

Order or news

You already know gelmemi söyledi — “told me to come.” That's an order. News is a different form.

ORDER · -mAk
NEWS · -DIk / -AcAk

An order looks ahead: do it. News looks at a fact: did it, will do it. That's why the tails differ.

Ask yourself: was I told to do something, or told about something? Told to do — -mAyI. Told about — -DIğI / -AcAğI.

Cheat sheet

Ali'nin geldiğini duydum
  • söyledi — said, duydum — heard, biliyorum — I know.
  • Someone else did it — they get -nin.
  • Pronouns get mirrored: seni → beni.
  • Told to — gelmemi. Told about — geldiğimi, geleceğimi.
  • “Not” lives inside the verb: gelmediğini, gelmeyeceğini.

That's it. Now you pass on gossip in Turkish. More carefully than the whole neighborhood.
