Passing on what other people said is the thing you do most all day. Who said what, who's going where.

Neighbor

You rent an apartment in Antalya. Your neighbor Murat is leaving for a day. On his way out he says: Yarın geleceğim. I'll be back tomorrow.

Landlady

In the evening the landlady asks: Murat ne dedi? What did Murat say?

You

You say: Murat yarın geleceğim söyledi. The landlady looks at you. Says: Sen mi geleceksin? So you're coming?

In the morning she bakes a pie. For you.

Guess: how do you report Murat's words?

From quote to story

Murat talks about himself. You talk about Murat. So the verb needs a change of clothes.

Five steps. Tap and watch what gets cut off and what sticks on.

The order is always the same: root, tense, whose, what, speech verb. No rearranging.

English swaps “I” for “he” too: “I’ll come” → “he’d come.” Turkish does the swap with a suffix.

Story 03English ↔ Türkçep. 05

Four tenses, two baskets

English does part of this too: “I’ll come” → “he said he’d come.” The “I” changes. Same idea.

But English also shifts the tense back: will → would, am → was, work → worked. Turkish shifts nothing. It asks one question: future or not?

Tap a quote. Watch four English forms drop into two Turkish baskets.

WORD ORDER

English: name, said, then a whole new sentence. Turkish packs that sentence into one word and puts “said” last.

Story 03Meet the speakersp. 05

Whose words

After the tense comes the “whose” tail. English puts a word in front: “I came,” “he came.” Turkish hangs it on the end: my, your, his.

Then one more -ı / -i / -u / -ü: the speech verb asks “what?” That's the accusative.

Tap a line to hear it. Hint: “his” and “your” have the same form. More on that in lesson two.

The tail shows who the news is about. Not who's telling it.

Story 04Refined detailsp. 06

Letters change

Four little things. Tap a card to see a letter change color.

You don't need to learn them separately. Say “yapdığını” out loud. Your tongue trips. Turkish just picks what's easy to say.

Say it right: c = j in “jam,” ğ is silent and just stretches the vowel, ı is like the a in “about.” So geleceğini sounds like “geh-leh-jeh-yee-nee.”

And negation: -me- goes right after the root. gelmediğini söyledi gelmeyeceğini söyledi

Cheat sheetSave as screenshotp. 12

Cheat sheet

geleceğini söyledi
  • Future — -(y)AcAğ. Everything else — -DIğ.
  • Then “whose”: -ım, -ın, -ı, -ımız, -ınız, -ları.
  • Then “what?”: -ı, and after “his” — -nı.
  • The speech verb is söyledi, and it goes last. dedi is for quotes.
  • No backshift: “would come” is still future — geleceğini.

Now you can report what people say. No quotation marks and no borrowed “I.”

Final.

The landlady asks again: Murat ne dedi?

You say: Yarın geleceğini söyledi. Now she's baking the pie for Murat. Fair enough.
