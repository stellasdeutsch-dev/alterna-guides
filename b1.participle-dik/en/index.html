Who does it? -(y)An or -DIk

the word itself does it

okuyan kitap

Guess first: in okuyan kız, who’s reading?

Use -(y)An when the word does it itself: okuyan kız — the girl who is reading. A book can’t read.

Someone else does it — you need -DIk. And the “who” sits in the ending: okuduğum kitap — the book I’m reading.

In okuduğum kitap, who’s reading?

Rule of the day

k + vowel → ğ

kitap → kitabı · okuduk → okuduğum

okuduğum kitap

Let’s build. The root oku, then -duk, then the “my” ending: -um.

k between vowels softens to ğ. Like kitap → kitabı. Same here: okuduk + um → okuduğum.

Okuduğum kitap. The book I’m reading. Three blocks — and no “which.”

Eight masks harmony and voiceless consonants

Word of the day

Top row — after voiced consonants and vowels; bottom row — after voiceless f, s, t, k, ç, ş, h, p.

The suffix has eight masks. The vowel follows harmony: sevdiğim, yazdığım, gördüğüm, okuduğum.

After voiceless consonants, d becomes t: içtiğim kahve, baktığım ev. Turks remember the voiceless ones with the phrase Fıstıkçı Şahap.

Sevdiğim yazar — the writer I love. İçtiğim kahve — the coffee I drink.

Yesterday or now -DIk doesn’t split time

14 days of reading

1234567 891011121314 15161718192021
dün okuduğum kitap
the book I read yesterday
şimdi okuduğum kitap
the book I’m reading now
tomorrow? a different form — episode 2

-DIk doesn’t split “yesterday” and “now.” Okuduğum kitap is both “that I’m reading” and “that I read.”

The words around it tell the time: dün okuduğum kitap — yesterday, şimdi okuduğum kitap — now.

The only thing it won’t do is the future. “That I will read” has its own form. That’s the next episode.

Remember

  • Does it itself — -(y)An. Someone does it — -DIk.
  • k before a vowel → ğ.
  • After voiceless consonants — t: içtiğim.
  • The noun without “my.”
