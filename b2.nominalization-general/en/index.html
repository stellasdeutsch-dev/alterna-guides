Scene 2Freeze frame

No glue

WHAT?
Sen kazandın.
that kazandığını bilmiyordum.

Freeze frame. In English, “that” glues the two sentences together. Here there’s no glue.

The second sentence gets packed into a capsule. A capsule is a noun.

You can know it, wait for it, fear it. Like a thing. Sen kazandın → kazandığın.

A whole sentence became one word. And that word sits calmly in front of the verb.

Guess first: where’s the capsule in Kazandığını bilmiyordum?

Scene 3root engine

The fact capsule’s engine

-DIK
kazandığın ödül
kazandığı
kazan·dığ·ıgel·diğ·ioku·duğ·ugör·düğ·üyap·tığ·ı

The fact capsule runs on -DIk. You’ve seen it before: kazandığın ödül — the prize you won.

Same -DIk here, only the word after it is gone. The fact itself became the object.

The vowel adjusts to the root: dık, dik, duk, dük. After voiceless sounds it’s t: yaptığı.

Before a vowel, k softens into ğ. A silent letter, but it pulls its weight.

Scene 4sticker

Whose fact is it

KİMİN?
Deniz'in kazandığı jeñgeniñ — Kazakh glues it on too

The capsule wears a sticker: whose fact it is. That’s the possessive suffix: -ım, -ın, -ı.

Whoever did it goes in the genitive: senin kazandığın, Deniz'in kazandığı.

Like “your win,” only about an action. Same as “Deniz’s winning” in English.

The capsule is ready. Which slot it goes into is up to the main verb.

bilmek takes -ı. sevinmek and inanmak take -a. haberi olmak takes -dan.

After the third-person -ı, an n steps in: kazandığı-n-dan. Without it the word trips.

Kazandığı___ haberim yoktu. Which slot?

At night Deniz texts: “The ceremony’s tomorrow evening. I want you to come.”

This isn’t a fact, it’s an action someone wants. Different engine: -mA.

It’s the -mA from yürümeyi seviyorum. Only now the action has an owner: gel-me-n-i.

What happened or is happening: -DIk. What people want, wait for, ask for: -mA.
