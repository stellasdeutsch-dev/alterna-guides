scene 2

bildiğim kadarıylaas far as I know

2 · kadarıyla
???

Let's take Ece's answer apart. Bil is “know.” -diğ makes a participle: “what I know.”

-im is whose knowledge. Mine. Then kadar, as in istediğin kadar, “as much as you want.”

And kadarıyla turns it into a frame: “within what I know.”

It's not a guarantee, it's a caveat. Ece honestly showed the limit of what she knows.

English has the same logic: “as far as I know” draws a line, it doesn't make a promise.

In the morning Arman writes to his manager: Ece'nin söylediğine göre webinar dokuzdaymış.

Söyle + diğ + i is “what she said.” Plus n + e, the dative, as in bana göre, “in my opinion.”

You get “by what Ece said.” In other words, according to Ece.

The -mış tail at the end is an honest label: I didn't see it myself, someone told me.

Whose words go in the genitive: Ece'nin. Now the source answers for the time, not Arman.

“According to Deniz Hoca, the recording will be posted tomorrow.”

Nine o'clock. The stream is on, and the chat explodes: ses yok, ses yok, ses yok.

Arman writes: Herkesin «ses yok» yazdığına göre mikrofon kapalı.

Here göre is no longer about someone's words. It's a conclusion: “judging by everyone writing…”

Fact on the left, conclusion on the right. Like a detective, only on Zoom. And no -mış: you draw the conclusion yourself.

For the future: -acağına göre: Webinarın dokuzda başlayacağına göre bir saatimiz var.

“Since Deniz Hoca is smiling, everything's fine.”

In the evening Arman puts together a cheat sheet of three tickets.

Source: duyduğuma göre kursa zam gelecekmiş — rumor has it the course will get more expensive.

Limit: duyduğum kadarıyla zam yok — as far as I've heard, there's no price hike.

Same verb, different job: göre passes on a rumor, kadarıyla adds a caveat.

Conclusion is the third ticket: fact plus göre, no -mış.

Which ticket do you need?

As far as I remember, Deniz Hoca is from Samsun.
According to Ece, the webinar was moved.

scene 6

sign-up formall persons

6 · forms

gör → …

gördüğüme göreI
gördüğüm kadarıylaI
gördüğüne göreyou
gördüğün kadarıylayou
gördüğüne görehe, she
gördüğü kadarıylahe, she
gördüklerine görethey
gördükleri kadarıylathey

Same participle as always: d after voiced sounds, t after voiceless ones, the vowel follows the root.

Before göre you need the dative: gördüğüme, gördüğüne. After “he/she” and “they” an n appears.

Trap: gördüğüne is both “by what you saw” and “by what he saw.” The genitive tells them apart: senin or onun.

Kadarıyla doesn't change at all. Only the person before it changes.

Build it: anla + kadarıyla, “we.”
