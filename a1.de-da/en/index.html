Rule 1 · meaning

The “too” balloon

English “too” and “also” can drift around the sentence. Turkish de can’t.

It sits right after the word. And says “too” about that word only.

Ayşe is a teacher. Ali too. So the balloon goes after Ali.

Loose word order won’t help you here. Turkish is an accountant: everything in its place.

Ayşe öğretmen. Ali de öğretmen.Ayşe is a teacher. Ali is a teacher too.
Ali de öğretmen.Ali is a teacher too.

Rule 2 · color

Balloon color

The balloon takes the color of the word’s last vowel. Not the first. Not your favorite. The last.

Final consonants don’t get a vote. Not even the loud ones.

e i ö üfront → de
a ı o uback → da

Quick questionÜsküdar ___ güzel.

Rule 3 · the space

On a string, not glued

The balloon is written separately. Always. Glue it on and you get a different word.

Ben de is “me too.” Bende is “on me.” The difference: one space and a whole life.

Ben deme too — on a string
Rule 4 · d only

No letter t

Case endings switch d to t after voiceless consonants. Our balloon doesn’t. It has principles. The letter t knocks. The door stays shut. Words can end as voiceless as they like — the balloon doesn’t care.

Mehmetde kebapda sütde tostda

Rule 5 · position

Where to tie it

The balloon says “too” about the word in front of it. Retie it and the meaning floats off with it.

One balloon — one “too.” Two balloons — that’s “both … and ….”

Çay da kahve de güzel.Both the tea and the coffee are good.
Ali de Ayşe de öğrenci.Both Ali and Ayşe are students.
