[ 01 ]Managed · couldn't

Yesterday: ir → di

The past is built the same way. Only ir is replaced by di. And in “couldn't”, bil drops out again.

In English, “could” is the past of “can” too. Same logic: the tense changes, the meaning stays put. The persons are like in the regular past.

bensenobizsizonlar

The most useful phrase of the week: Dün gelemedim, özür dilerim. — “I couldn't make it yesterday, sorry”.

[ 03 ]Might not

Might not

A third look-alike form. The negation goes before -yebil, not instead of it. You get “I might not come”. A polite way to promise nothing.

[ 04 ]Maybe · can't be

-abil guesses

-abil has one more job — guessing. olmak + abil = olabilir, “maybe”. Tap a phrase and watch the needle.

English “may be” comes from “may” — a word for being able. Turkish thinks the same way. The further right the needle, the stronger the certainty.

Evde olabilir.Maybe he's home.

Works as one word too: Olabilir. — “Maybe”. Olamaz! — “No way!”
