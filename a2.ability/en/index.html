[ 00 ]First week

First week of Turkish classes. A hoca — the teacher — is standing at the board.

She looks at you and says: Tahtaya gelebilir misin?

You know gel. That's “come here”. The rest is noise.

Guess what you answered.

You say: Gelemem. She says: Neden? You: Bilmiyorum.

The class laughs. You laugh too.

You meant to say “I didn't get the question”. What you said: “I can't come to the board. Why — no idea”.

The board was three meters away.

By the end of the lesson it'll be clear what you said back then. And what you should have said.

[ 01 ]Can · know how · may

One suffix — can

In English, “can” is a separate word before the verb. In Turkish it lives inside the verb, like a brick in a wall. Examples first. Tap and listen.

There's always bil in the middle. Before it — a or e. After it — ir and the person.

Formula · build it step by step

  • a or e — depends on the root's last vowel: yapabilirim, gelebilirim.
  • Root ends in a vowel — add y: okuyabilirim, bekleyebilirim.
  • git and et turn t into d before a vowel: gidebilirim.
  • bil itself never changes.
How to remember

bil comes from the verb bilmek, “to know”. If you know how, you can.

[ 02 ]May I? · could you?

Question = politeness

Add mi — and “you can” turns into a request. In Turkish government offices nobody gives orders. Everything goes through -ebilir misiniz?

An order sounds rude even with “please”. A request as a question is soft and grown-up.

Bekleyin. Wait.

The particle mi is written separately and takes the person: gelebilir miyim, gelebilir misiniz.

[ 03 ]Can't · bil disappears

Can't: bil disappears

Almost everyone trips here. You want to say “gelebilmem”. You can't. In the negative, bil drops out.

Hit “Break” — and watch what's left.

What's left is a/e, then the negation ma/me, then the negative aorist. Tap the form.

For biz, z turns into y: gelemeyiz. It's highlighted.

How to remember: if you can't, there's nothing to know. bil leaves along with the skill.
