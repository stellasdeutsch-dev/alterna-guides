the runaway letter

A vowel shows up — k leaves

-mAk has a habit. It sees a case vowel — it leaves. y takes its place. It sees a consonant — it stays. Like a neighbor on the stairs.

+ı
-ı · -avowel: k leaves, y steps in
-tanconsonant: k stays

Try all three cases. With the accusative and dative, k flies off. With the ablative it doesn’t even move. Between two vowels you need a buffer, and k makes a bad buffer.

Every verb wants its own

The main verb is a magnet. It decides which case to pull. That’s not logic, that’s personality. Tap a card — the magnet kicks in.

Memorize them in threes. Love and forget — what. Start and decide — to what. Fear and be sick — of what.

English helps here. No little word (love it, forget it) → accusative. “to” (start to, decide to) → dative. “of” (afraid of, sick of) → ablative. One catch: in “love to swim” and “forgot to lock” the “to” doesn’t count — those are still accusative.

Now with a person and three cases

Same rule: person, then case. Want — what. Glad — about what. Afraid — of what. For “his” and “their” there’s a buffer n before any case.
