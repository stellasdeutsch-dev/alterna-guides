01 · Story

Siz şeker at eight a.m.

1

You're at a tea house in Kadıköy. Morning. You want tea without sugar.

2

You say, çay, siz şeker. Confidently. Like you studied.

3

The waiter smiles. Blushes. Brings the tea. And two sugar cubes.

4

Because siz şeker means “you are sugar.” A compliment. Before breakfast. The waiter is your friend now.

03 · Notice it yourself

Six pairs. What do they share?

On the left, something is there. On the right, it isn't. Look at the word endings.

withwithout

The word stays whole. A tail hangs off the end: -lı — with, -sız — without.

English needs a separate word here: with, without. Turkish just hangs a tail. Like a tag on a suitcase. One word — two meanings. The tail decides. (English -less, as in sugarless, is a close cousin of -sız.)

04 · The rule

How the tail sticks

Four steps. Tap “next.” Or turn on autopilot and watch the letters drop on their own. You don't choose the tail's vowel. You copy it.

  1. Take the whole word. Don't touch it.
  2. Find the last vowel.
  3. Attach the tail: with — l, without — s.
  4. The tail's vowel repeats the word's last vowel.

Eight forms. One logic.

The tail's vowel is an echo of the word's last vowel. Just like four-way harmony.

08 · Compare

English ↔ Turkish

tea without sugar

In English, “without” goes before the noun, as a separate word, and it all comes after the tea.

In Turkish the tail goes at the end. The word doesn't change. And the whole pair moves in front of the tea: şekersiz çay.

Know Kazakh? Same thing there: qantsyz şai. A tail, attached, before the tea.

09 · Remember

L — loaded.
S — swiped.

şekerli — the glass is loaded with sugar. şekersiz — someone swiped the sugar. Before you got there.

Silly? Yes. But in a week it'll pop up on its own. Tested on sugar. The letter L looks like a spoon in a glass. S looks like the tail of a runaway sugar cube.

13 · Traps

Where everyone trips

The tail is written attached. A separate siz means “you.”

sütlı

Look at the word's vowel, not at habit. süt has ü.

limonli

Only the last vowel counts. Here it's o; the first i stays quiet.
