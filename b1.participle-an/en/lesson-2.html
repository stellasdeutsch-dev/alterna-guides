Catamaran

Episode two. I made it onto the catamaran after all. Wind, sea, sunglasses on my head.

A minute later the glasses are gone. I run to the captain. I say: Adam... şapka var... gözlük aldı!

The captain says: Burada herkesin şapkası var. Everyone here has a hat. You'll need a better description.

Scene 2

Stamp -mA-

yüzen yüzmeyen

Rule: stem + -mA- + y + -An. The stamp means “not.”

Some passengers jump into the sea. yüzen turistler — tourists who swim.

The rest sit on deck. We put on the black stamp -mA-: yüzmeyen turistler.

After the stamp there's always the fender y: gelmeyen, okumayan. Stem, stamp, y, tail.

Switch the passengers on deck.

Scene 3

Have and don't

Adamın şapkası var. şapkası olan adam gözlüğü olmayan adam

Rule: var turns into olan, yok into olmayan.

The first suspect wears a hat. Adamın şapkası var. The man has a hat.

But var doesn't turn into a sign. Instead of var we say olan, instead of yok — olmayan.

şapkası olan adam — the man with a hat. gözlüğü olmayan adam — that's me.

The sign: a woman who has a camera.

Scene 4

N noun

Siyah bir gözlük gören var mı?

gören kişi yüzenlere

Rule: with no word after it, the sign itself means “the one who…” and takes -ler and cases.

The captain grabs a megaphone: Siyah bir gözlük gören var mı? Has anyone seen black glasses?

There's no word “person.” The sign hangs in the air and becomes a word itself: gören — the one who saw.

From there it works like any word: görenler, yüzenlere havlu — towels for the swimmers.

Build: to those who swim. Tap in order.

yüzen

Scene 5

Time

Şimdi gelen tekne

the boat coming in now

Rule: the sign has no tense of its own. The words around it give the time.

The sign doesn't wear a watch.

Dün gelen turist — the tourist who arrived yesterday. Şimdi gelen tekne — the boat coming in now.

Her sabah gelen martı — the seagull that flies in every morning. The neighboring words tell the time.

Spin the time — the sign doesn't change.

Scene 6

Moody ones

yiyen simityiyenmartı

Rule: just memorize these four verbs — there aren't many.

A seagull sits on the mast eating someone's simit.

ye — to eat, the sign is yiyen. And de → diyen. Here e turns into i.

And git and et swap t for d: giden, yardım eden. Simit yiyen martı — the seagull eating simit.

Tap each verb to see its sign.

Scene 7

Long sign

her sabah teknede simit yiyen martı

Rule: everything that specifies the action goes before the sign.

You can make the sign longer. Everything new goes on the left.

teknede simit yiyen martı — the seagull eating simit on the boat.

Her sabah teknede simit yiyen martı. The longer the sign, the later the seagull itself shows up.

Make it longer yourself: add details one by one.

yiyenmartı
